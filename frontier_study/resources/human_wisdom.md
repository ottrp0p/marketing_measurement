# Resource Library — Human Understanding & Marketing Wisdom

Annotated bibliography for the behavioral, brand, and consumer-psychology side of measurement. Books are also indexed in `books_and_videos.md`.

## Empirical laws of brand growth & critiques

1. **How Brands Grow: What Marketers Don't Know** — Byron Sharp (2010, book). Canonical statement of marketing's empirical laws (double jeopardy, penetration, Dirichlet). Institute hub: https://marketingscience.info/
2. **How Brands Grow Part 2** (rev. ed.) — Romaniuk & Sharp (2021, book). Extends the laws to emerging markets, services, luxury.
3. **Better Brand Health: Measures and Metrics for a How Brands Grow World** — Jenni Romaniuk (2023, book). The current playbook for CEP-based brand tracking. https://www.oup.com.au/books/higher-education/management-and-marketing/9780190340919-better-brand-health-ebook
4. **Answering Critics** — Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (ongoing). The institute's rebuttals to the main critiques; a map of the debate. https://marketingscience.info/answering-critics/
5. **Double Jeopardy — 50 Years On** — John Dawes (2017/2020). Modern restatement and validation of DJ as a benchmarking tool. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1441358217301519
6. **Wine-retail replication of DJ & duplication-of-purchase laws** — Dawes et al. (2023). Example of the ongoing replication program. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374193474
7. **Empirical generalisations in customer mindset metrics** — Mecredy, Wright & Feetham (2022). Extends empirical-generalization discipline to attitudinal tracking. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cb.1961
8. **"Byron Sharp ignores 60 years of published work"** — Felipe Thomaz via Contagious (2024). Sharpest current academic critique of the Ehrenberg-Bass program. https://www.contagious.com/en/article/news-and-views/byron-sharp-how-brands-grow-ignores-60-years-of-published-work-felipe-thomaz
9. **"Really mediocre outcomes" — Thomaz on E-B rules** — Mi3 (2024). 1,000-campaign evidence claim against mechanical reach-maximization. https://www.mi-3.com.au/15-10-2024/really-mediocre-outcomes-oxford-uni-professor-says-byron-sharp-and-ehrenberg-bass
10. **The "wiggle room" in marketing science** — Contagious (2024). Balanced view: laws constrain but don't determine creative strategy. https://www.contagious.com/news-and-views/creativity-marketing-science-sharp-how-brands-grow-wiggle-room
11. **Category Entry Points guide** — Jenni Romaniuk (2023). Author's explanation of CEP measurement for mental market share. http://www.jenniromaniuk.com/blog/2023/1/19/increasing-mental-market-share-by-using-category-entry-points

## Long vs short term & effectiveness

12. **The Long and the Short of It** — Binet & Field, IPA (2013, book/report). Source of the brand/activation distinction and 60:40. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/effectiveness-research-analysis/les-binet-peter-field
13. **Effectiveness in Context** — Binet & Field, IPA (2018). Refines 60:40 by category, brand size, online share.
14. **Profit Ability 2: The New Business Case for Advertising** — Thinkbox/Ebiquity/Gain Theory et al. (2024). £1.8bn, 141 brands: ~60% of ad-driven profit lands after week 13. https://www.thinkbox.tv/research/thinkbox-research/profit-ability-2-the-new-business-case-for-advertising · PDF summary: https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/AOA1%20-%20Thinkbox,%20Ebiquity,%20EssenceMediacom,%20Gain%20Theory,%20Mindshare%20&%20Wavemaker%20UK.pdf
15. **Long-term advertising effects: the Adstock illusion** — Peter Cain, Marketscience (2024). Demolishes long-decay adstock as a brand-effect measure; proposes baseline-evolution decomposition. https://market.science/modelling-the-long-term-the-adstock-illusion/
16. **Making measurement triangulation work** — Matt Andrew (Ekimetrics) for IPA (2023). Practitioner triangulation doctrine; names the organizational blockers. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/making-measurement-triangulation-work
17. **Les Binet on measurement in the digital age** — Google/Think (2024). Binet's current guidance on combining MMM, experiments, and judgment. https://business.google.com/uk/think/measurement/les-binet-marketing-measurement/

## What advertising does: elasticities, theory, memory

18. **How Well Does Advertising Work? Generalizations from Meta-Analysis of Brand Advertising Elasticities** — Sethuraman, Tellis & Briesch (JMR 2011). The anchor priors: short-run elasticity ~0.12, long-run ~0.24. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.48.3.457 · PDF: https://gwern.net/doc/economics/advertising/2011-sethuraman.pdf
19. **Advertising: Strong Force or Weak Force? Two Views an Ocean Apart** — John Philip Jones (IJA 1990). Frames the persuasion-vs-reinforcement debate. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02650487.1990.11107151
20. **Repetitive Advertising and the Consumer (ATR/ATR-N)** — Andrew Ehrenberg (1974/2000, JAR). The weak-force theory: ads nudge and reinforce rather than convert. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4854429
21. **Recency & Receptivity — lessons from Erwin Ephron** — Brandingmag (2021). Recency planning: the media-planning translation of memory-refresh theory. https://www.brandingmag.com/2021/10/26/recency-receptivity-marketing-lessons-from-erwin-ephron/
22. **Memory Enhances the Mere Exposure Effect** — Stafford & Grimes (Psychology & Marketing 2012). Ties mere exposure to explicit memory — low-attention ad processing. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.20581
23. **Wearout or Weariness?** — Chae, Bruno & Feinberg (~2023). Evidence on negative frequency effects (retargeting fatigue). PDF: https://www.hernanbruno.com/uploads/Chae_Bruno_Feinberg.pdf

## Experiments, heterogeneity, incrementality (human-side)

24. **The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising** — Lewis & Rao (QJE 2015). Human purchase variance makes ad ROI nearly unmeasurable at typical scale. https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/130/4/1941/1914592 · PDF: https://gwern.net/doc/economics/advertising/2015-lewis.pdf
25. **Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness** — Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (Econometrica 2015). Selection masquerading as ad effect; returns concentrated in light users. PDF: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20171/w20171.pdf
26. **Ghost Ads** — Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer (JMR 2017). The standard fix for control-group contamination in lift measurement. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2620078
27. **Inferno: A guide to field experiments in online display advertising** — Garrett Johnson (JEMS 2023). Catalog of the ways ad experiments go wrong. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jems.12513
28. **Heterogeneous treatment effects and optimal targeting policy evaluation** — (QME 2024). State of the art on estimating who responds. https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/qmktec/v22y2024i2d10.1007_s11129-023-09278-5.html
29. **The effectiveness of nudging: meta-analysis** — Mertens et al. (PNAS 2022). Headline nudge effect sizes (pre-correction). https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118
30. **No reason to expect large and consistent effects of nudge interventions** — Maier, Szaszi et al. (PNAS 2022). The publication-bias correction that shrank nudging — cautionary tale for vendor meta-claims. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2200732119

## Attention, creative, emotion

31. **The Attention Economy and How Media Works** — Karen Nelson-Field (2020, book). Foundational cross-platform attention evidence; why viewability misleads. https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-15-1540-8
32. **The Art of Attention: findings that matter most** — Nelson-Field / AANA (2020). Digest of the core attention-economy findings. https://aana.com.au/2020/09/04/the-art-of-attention-findings-that-matter-most-to-marketers/
33. **Attention metrics explained: Lumen, Adelaide** — VISTY (2024–25). Comparison of the two leading commercial attention methodologies. https://visty.io/en-us/post/attention-metrics-explained-lumen-adelaide-and-how-they-work
34. **The Great Attention Metrics Battle** — ADOTAT (2025). Critical view of vendor disagreement — the convergent-validity problem. https://new.adotat.com/p/the-great-attention-metrics-battle-adelaide-vs-lumen-vs-everyone-else
35. **ARF ATTENTION program** — Advertising Research Foundation (2024–25). The industry's neutral validation effort for attention measurement. https://thearf.org/arf-events/attention-2025/
36. **Lemon: How the Advertising Brain Turned Sour** — Orlando Wood, IPA (2019, book). Left/right-brain account of the creative-effectiveness decline.
37. **Look out** — Orlando Wood, IPA (2021, book). Attention, characters, and "broad-beam" creative as the fix. https://ipa.co.uk/news/look-out
38. **The Art of Proof: how creative quality drives profit** — Kantar (2024). Links pre-tested creative quality to profit ROI at database scale. https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/the-art-of-proof-how-creative-quality-drives-profit
39. **Marketers understate creative, overestimate targeting** — Westwood One / Nielsen-NCS synthesis (2024). Creative ≈ half of ad-driven sales. https://www.westwoodone.com/blog/2024/04/22/marketers-vastly-understate-the-sales-effect-of-creative-and-significantly-overestimate-the-impact-of-targeting/
40. **The Extraordinary Cost of Dull** — Field, Morgan & Evans (System1, 2023–25). Dull ads need 2–2.6x the media budget — emotion quantified in money. https://system1group.com/the-extraordinary-cost-of-dull · PDF: https://radiopub.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/01.27.25-Dullness-Of-Ads.pdf
41. **Automatic facial coding predicts ad and brand effects** — Höfling & Alpers (Frontiers in Neuroscience 2023). Best recent peer-reviewed validation of facial coding for video ads. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2023.1125983/full
42. **Compounding creative** — Mi3 on System1/Kantar/Analytic Partners/Arnott's (2025). Hard numbers on consistent creative platforms vs. switching. https://www.mi-3.com.au/22-01-2025/compounding-creative-system1-arnotts-specsavers-kantar-and-analytics-partners-holding

## LLMs & synthetic consumers

43. **Large Language Models as Simulated Economic Agents (Homo Silicus)** — John Horton, MIT (2023). Founding paper for LLMs as behavioral-economics subjects. https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07543
44. **Using LLMs for Market Research** — Brand, Israeli & Ngwe, HBS (2023–25). GPT conjoint/WTP estimates track human demand curves. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4395751 · PDF: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/23-062_6bfe7a5b-3ed9-4afd-a4c1-c91b60dd82e5.pdf
45. **Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People** — Park et al., Stanford/DeepMind (2024). Interview-grounded agents hit 85% of humans' own test-retest reliability. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109 · code: https://github.com/joonspk-research/genagents
46. **Take caution in using LLMs as human surrogates** — Gao, Lee, Burtch & Fazelpour (PNAS 2025). Key negative result: LLMs reason unlike humans; fragile to perturbation. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2501660122 · arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19599
47. **LLMs cannot replace human participants (identity portrayal)** — Wang, Morgenstern & Dickerson (2024). Flattened variance and identity misportrayal. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01908
48. **Synthetic replacements for human survey data: the perils of LLMs** — Bisbee et al. (Political Analysis 2024). Instability of silicon samples across prompts and time.
49. **Can AI language models replace human participants?** — Dillion, Tandon, Gu & Gray (TiCS 2023). Early balanced framework for when LLM simulation is admissible. https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/abstract/S1364-6613(23)00098-0
50. **LLMs can predict the results of social science experiments** — Hewitt, Ashokkumar, Ghezae & Willer (Nature 2026). Strongest positive evidence for LLM forecasting of human experimental outcomes. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10742-x
51. **Correcting Mode Collapse in Silicon Sampling with Semantic Similarity Rating** — (2026). Current best-practice technique for realistic response distributions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28550
52. **Mitigating Social Desirability Bias in Random Silicon Sampling** — (2025). Documents and corrects a systematic human-likeness failure. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22725
53. **Synthetic Consumers in Market Research: A Practical Guide** — PyMC Labs (2026). Best practitioner synthesis: validation stats, methods, limits. https://www.pymc-labs.com/blog-posts/synthetic-consumers-a-practical-guide
54. **AI–Human Hybrids for Marketing Research** — Arora, Chakraborty & Nishimura (Journal of Marketing 2025). The "LLM as collaborator, not replacement" framework. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00222429241276529
55. **Automated Social Science: LLMs as Scientist and Subjects** — Manning, Zhu & Horton (NBER w32381, 2024). Where simulation is heading: hypothesis generation + testing loops. PDF: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32381/w32381.pdf
