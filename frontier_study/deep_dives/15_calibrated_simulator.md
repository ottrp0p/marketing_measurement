## 1. Problem statement

E9 asks for "a calibrated simulator whose counterfactual predictions match held-out geo-experiment results at scale — the necessary bar before simulation can substitute for any experiment"; H7 asks for a theory of *when* synthetic consumers are valid. Both catalog entries name the bar without defining it. This dive defines it quantitatively, derives what a library of real experiments can certify about a simulator and at what cost, and specifies the harness that does the certifying. The simulator is treated as a black box that emits a counterfactual prediction; nothing in the mathematics depends on it being an LLM, and that is the point — the certificate must be earned against experiments, not argued from behavioral realism.

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Library.** E past randomized experiments e = 1..E (geo lift tests, ghost-ad holdouts) with true lift τ_e and estimate τ̂_e = τ_e + ε_e, ε_e ~ N(0, v_e), the sampling variances v_e known from the experiment's own analysis and independent across experiments (A1).
- **Simulator.** For each experiment the simulator is run on the pre-period description and emits s_e, its counterfactual lift prediction, before the experiment reads out (A2: pre-registration; §3.3 shows what happens without it).
- **Recalibration model.** τ_e = α + βs_e + u_e, u_e ~ N(0, ω²) independent of s_e (A3: linear, homoscedastic). ω² is the simulator's **counterfactual residual variance** — the irreducible error that remains after the best affine recalibration. It is the single number the harness must estimate, and the object nobody in the literature attaches a decision-theoretic threshold to (§2).
- **Target decision.** A new channel decision with the simulator's recalibrated prediction m = α̂ + β̂s_new and predictive variance V. Two loss models: quadratic (continuous allocation, profit τx − γx²/2, loss (τ − x̂γ)²/2γ) and threshold (go/no-go at τ₀, loss |τ − τ₀| on the wrong side). An experiment for the target costs c and returns x = τ + N(0, v_x).
- **Contamination.** A fraction λ of library items may be *memorised*: the simulator returns the published estimate τ̂_e rather than a prediction (A2 violated).
- **Structural sub-model (§3.4).** Purchase iff U_i + a_i d + η_i > 0 with U_i ~ N(μ, σ_H²), η_i ~ N(0,1), dose d, sensitivity a_i = a for a responsive fraction q and 0 otherwise; s := √(1+σ_H²). Truth lift L(μ, d) = q[Φ((μ + ad)/s) − Φ(μ/s)]. A *homogeneous* agent simulator has no idiosyncratic dispersion: it matches the baseline level (latent mean μ′ = Φ⁻¹(p₀)) and predicts Φ(μ′ + a′d) − Φ(μ′).

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run). The empirical frontier is close to the setting but stops short of a certificate. Ashokkumar–Hewitt–Ghezae–Willer (Nature 2026) predict 476 survey-experiment effects with GPT-4 at r = 0.85 (0.90 on unpublished studies), with systematic magnitude over-estimation and much lower r for field experiments (0.27). Hut & Masoero (Amazon, arXiv 2608.02345, Aug 2026) simulate 67 marketing A/B tests with 1,000 customer-mapped agents: sign agreement 0.70, launch-decision alignment 0.41, and — the closest object to this dive — a "corrected MSE" that subtracts each test's sampling variance from the squared residual (the DerSimonian–Laird moment idea), which a Platt-scaling recalibration on 16 tests reduces ~77× "to near the noise floor". Toubia et al.'s Twin-2K-500 and the Peng–Toubia mega-study (19 preregistered studies, 164 outcomes) find twins less variable than humans and only about half of aggregate treatment effects replicated; Bisbee et al. (Political Analysis 2024) measure variance collapse (16.1 vs 31.4); Gui & Toubia (2023) show prompt-manipulation confounding; two 2026 preprints (arXiv 2604.02458, 2608.00979) show descriptive fit does not predict causal-effect error. Statistical ingredients exist separately: τ² estimators and prediction intervals in meta-analysis (Veroniki et al. 2016; Riley–Higgins–Deeks 2011), prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al. 2023) and mixed-subjects designs (Broska et al. 2025), Spotify's surrogacy framing of LLM A/B tests (arXiv 2606.17165), Kennedy–O'Hagan discrepancy calibration, the reusable holdout (Dwork et al. 2015), and LLM contamination tests that need logprobs (Oren et al. 2024) or recall probes (arXiv 2606.13670). The subagent found none of: (a) a bar on the residual variance tied to experiment cost and decision value; (b) a sample-size law for the number of certifying experiments; (c) a noise-floor contamination test formalised against noisy RCT labels; (d) a structural (heterogeneity-inflation) recalibration validated for transport. Those four are the constructions below.

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — the excess-variance certificate and the certification law (established here; clean-room confirmed)

**Estimator.** Regress τ̂ on (1, s); with residuals r, leverages h_ee, the statistic

  ω̂² = [r′r − Σ_e (1 − h_ee) v_e]/(E − 2)

is *exactly* unbiased for ω² (r = (I−H)(u+ε), E[r′r] = ω²(E−2) + Σ(1−h_ee)v_e), and for constant v, r′r = (ω²+v)χ²_{E−2}, so sd(ω̂²) = (ω²+v)√(2/(E−2)). Hut & Masoero's corrected MSE is the same object without the leverage term and without the recalibration degrees of freedom. E1 (4,000 reps, ω = 0.5, v = 0.8): mean 0.2395/0.2530/0.2496/0.2478/0.2494 at E = 8/15/30/60/120 (truth 0.25; MC se ≤ 0.009), sd 0.594/0.418/0.275/0.196/0.137 vs formula 0.606/0.412/0.281/0.195/0.137; P(ω̂² < 0) = 0.41 at E = 8 and 0.18 at E = 30 — the estimator is honest but *noisy*: at E = 30 its sd is larger than the quantity it estimates. Clean-room: 0.2558/0.2543/0.2483 with sds 0.603/0.281/0.136.

**Certificate.** UCB_{1−a} = (E−2)(ω̂² + v̄)/χ²_{a,E−2} − v̄; certify "ω² ≤ ω*²" iff UCB ≤ ω*². Coverage 0.950/0.949/0.955/0.949/0.950 across E (E1).

**Certification law.** Write ρ = ω²/ω*² (how good the simulator really is relative to the bar) and ν = v̄/ω*² (how noisy the library experiments are relative to the bar). The certification event is (ω̂²+v̄)/(ω²+v̄) ≤ κ·χ²_{a,E−2}/(E−2) with the **gap ratio κ = (1+ν)/(ρ+ν)**, and the left side is χ²_{E−2}/(E−2). Hence

  **power = P(χ²_{E−2} ≤ κ χ²_{a,E−2}); the library size needed for power 1−b is the smallest E with χ²_{1−b,E−2}/χ²_{a,E−2} ≤ κ,**
  **E − 2 ≈ 2(z_b + κz_a)²/(κ−1)² = 2(z_b + κz_a)²(ρ+ν)²/(1−ρ)²**  (normal approximation).

E2 (3,000 reps per cell, bisection on E): the exact law gives 14/31/62/157 (ρ = 0.25; ν = 0.1/0.5/1/2), 39/82/157/383 (ρ = 0.5), 129/258/480/1130 (ρ = 0.7); Monte Carlo E for power 0.8: 13/31/62/155, 40/81/157/397, 131/261/465/1109 — every cell within MC resolution, power at E_exact 0.79–0.83. Clean-room: (0.5, 0.5) → 82, MC 0.798; (0.25, 1.0) → 62, MC 0.807. Reading the law: *everything depends on κ alone*; certifying a simulator that is half as bad as the bar (ρ = 0.5) with library experiments as noisy as the bar (ν = 1) takes **157 experiments**; with experiments four times more precise (ν = 0.25) it takes ≈ 55; a simulator at ρ = 0.7 with ν = 1 takes 480. Hut & Masoero's 67 tests can therefore certify only a bar that the simulator beats by a wide margin.

**Library precision, not count.** If experiment cost is ∝ 1/v (geo-weeks), total certification cost ∝ E(ρ, ν)/ν. E2: the minimiser is ν* ≈ 0.81–0.91 for ρ = 0.25–0.7, and the cost surface is flat there (cost(ν*)/cost(ν = 1) = 0.99–1.00) while over-precise libraries are wasteful (ν = 0.1 costs 2.6–2.8× more). Rule: **run the library experiments at roughly the precision of the bar you want to certify, v̄ ≈ ω*²**. (Round 1 had derived ν* = ρ from the wrong law; see §4.)

### 3.2 Construction 2 — the counterfactual bar (established here; per-decision form clean-room confirmed)

The bar is not a correlation or a sign-agreement rate; it is the residual variance at which a fresh experiment stops paying for itself. With predictive variance V = ω̂² + (ω̂² + v̄)[1/E + (s_new − s̄)²/S_ss] and an experiment of variance v_x, the posterior variance is (1/V + 1/v_x)⁻¹ and under quadratic loss

  EVSI_quad(V) = V²/[2γ(V + v_x)]  (E3a: MC 0.0251/0.0836/0.2481/0.6642 vs closed 0.0250/0.0833/0.2500/0.6667),

so the experiment is worth running iff EVSI > c, i.e. the simulator substitutes iff

  **V ≤ ω*² := γc + √(γ²c² + 2γc·v_x).**

In units of v_x with c̃ = c/(v_x/2γ) (experiment cost relative to the loss a one-experiment-wide prior carries): ω*²/v_x = 0.25/0.37/0.64/1.00/1.62 at c̃ = 0.05/0.1/0.25/0.5/1; equivalently the simulator must be worth 4.0/2.7/1.6/1.0/0.6 **experiment-equivalents** EE := v_x/ω². A simulator that is exactly as informative as one experiment (EE = 1) clears the bar only when the experiment costs half the loss its own width carries.

**The per-decision bar (Round 3).** For threshold decisions the quadratic bar is wrong by a factor that depends on the decision (E3c: ratio EVSI_thresh/EVSI_quad from 3.5 at m = 0, V = 0.25 down to 0.00 at m = 1, V = 0.25). The exact preposterior value is

  **EVSI_thresh(m, V) = σ_pp·ψ(|m − τ₀|/σ_pp), σ_pp = V/√(V + v_x), ψ(z) = φ(z) − z(1 − Φ(z)),**

(E12a: 0.2821/0.0998/0.1234 closed vs 0.2800/0.0989/0.1237 MC; clean-room 0.2822/0.1000/0.1230 ± 0.0003), so the harness should emit a *per-decision* verdict "experiment iff σ_pp ψ(|m−τ₀|/σ_pp) > c" — dive 08's probability-of-regret logic with the simulator as the prior. E12b (go/no-go stream, c = 0.05, E = 60): at ω² = 0.5 (above the global bar 0.37, so the global rule runs every experiment at loss 0.1122) the per-decision rule runs only 14% of experiments at loss 0.0831 vs oracle 0.0805 and simulator-only 0.0964; at ω² = 0.2 both rules certify and match the oracle (0.0393 vs 0.0390); at ω² = 1–2 both fall back to always-experiment (0.139/0.140), 12–15% above an oracle that still skips 17% of experiments because it knows ω² exactly.

### 3.3 Construction 3 — contamination: the noise-floor test, its blind spot, and the re-estimation test (established here; blind spot clean-room confirmed)

**Noise-floor test.** An honest simulator cannot predict the sampling noise ε_e, so E[r′r] ≥ Σ(1−h_ee)v_e; under H₀: ω = 0 and homoscedastic v, r′r/Σ(1−h)v ~ χ²_{E−2}/(E−2). Reject "honest" when r′r < χ²_{a,E−2}/(E−2)·Σ(1−h)v — a residual variance *below the label noise* is proof of label memorisation. A memoriser returning τ̂_e on a fraction λ has expected residual variance (1−λ)(ω²+v), so the test has power only when (1−λ)(1+ω²/v) < 1 − z_a√(2/(E−2)): **the blind spot is λ < ω²/(ω²+v)** — a simulator with real error ω² can hide a memorised fraction up to ω²/(ω²+v) of the library. E6a (E = 30, v = 1): rejection at λ = 0/0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8/1 is 0.05/0.22/0.54/0.85/0.98/1.00 at ω = 0, 0.01/0.09/0.32/0.70/0.95/1.00 at ω = 0.5 (boundary 0.2), 0.00/0.01/0.06/0.32/0.79/1.00 at ω = 1 (boundary 0.5); clean-room 0.011/0.073/0.303/0.714/0.976/1.000 and 0.000/0.003/0.036/0.283/0.825/1.000. **Strategic evasion** (E6b): a memoriser that adds N(0, v + ω²) noise to the published estimate passes the floor test (rejection 0.18 ≈ the honest rate at that apparent ω) and presents as an honest ω = 0.5 simulator. The floor test is necessary, never sufficient.

**Re-estimation test** (needs the raw experiment data, which an advertiser-run harness has). Re-estimate every library lift with a second valid estimator τ̂′_e = τ_e + ε′_e (different control-geo subset, different covariate adjustment, CUPED vs raw), corr(ε, ε′) = r < 1. Define D_e = (s_e − τ̂′_e)² − (s_e − τ̂_e)² − (v′_e − v_e). An honest simulator has E[D_e] = 0 whatever its ω; a memoriser of fraction λ has E[D_e] = 2λv(1 − r) > 0, and *adding noise does not help it* because its output still tracks ε rather than ε′. E6c (E = 30, one-sided t-test): power 0.05/0.09/0.15/0.35/1.00 at λ = 0/0.1/0.2/0.4/1 for r = 0.5 (0.04/0.07/0.11/0.23/1.00 at r = 0.8); E6d: the noise-adding memoriser that fooled the floor test is caught with power 0.71 (r = 0.5) / 0.40 (r = 0.8). Power is weak for partial contamination at E = 30 and scales with E(1 − r)²; the harness should choose the re-estimator to minimise r (disjoint control geos).

### 3.4 Construction 4 — which recalibration family transports (established here; clean-room confirmed)

Homogeneity ("variance collapse") is the most-cited LLM failure mode. In the structural sub-model it has a precise consequence. With q = 1 (everyone responsive), Φ(μ/s + (a/s)d) − Φ(μ/s) ≡ Φ((μ+ad)/s) − Φ(μ/s): **a homogeneous simulator recalibrated on the latent scale with one slope a″ = a/s is exact at every baseline and dose** (clean-room max error 1.1e−16). Variance collapse in the *level* is therefore harmless once recalibration happens inside the link — but the *additive* lift-scale recalibration α + βs (Platt-style) is not: E5 (24-experiment library, doses 0.3–1.0, baselines μ ∈ [−1.5, −0.5], 200k users per arm, 300 reps): in-range relative rmse 0.022 (additive) vs 0.002 (latent), but at dose 2.5 the additive family errs −15.7% and at a rare baseline μ = −3 by +14.5%, while the latent family stays within 0.3%. With q = 0.4 (a responsive subpopulation — heterogeneity in *sensitivity*, not level) the one-parameter latent family fails too: +3.8% at dose 2.5 and **−12.7% at μ = −3** (clean-room −13.0%/+3.5%), and only the two-parameter mixture family q′[Φ(μ′+a″d) − Φ(μ′)] transports (rmse 0.033/0.043; clean-room recovers q′ = 0.400, a″ = 0.500 exactly). The additive family with the same two parameters fails at −15.9%/+15.2%. The lesson for H7: *what* the simulator gets wrong determines the family the harness must fit, and the certificate is only valid inside the library's (dose, baseline) hull unless the family is structural. E4 makes the factual/counterfactual point directly: in a random-utility simulator, corr(level error, lift error) = +0.02, and a simulator with 6× worse level fit but the right sensitivity has 4.6× smaller lift error (0.019 vs 0.087).

### 3.5 Distribution shift, selection, and sequential certification (established here)

- **Shift.** With ω²(x) = ω₀² + κx² in a novelty covariate x and a library concentrated near x = 0 (E7, E = 60): the global prediction interval covers 0.999 at x = 0 (over-wide), 0.94 at x = 1, 0.71 at x = 2, 0.54 at x = 3; a kernel-local excess-variance estimate restores 0.93/0.92/0.86/0.80 with n_eff 36/47/18/4. Certification is *local*: outside the library's coverage the harness has no certificate to give, and H7's "genuinely new products" case is exactly x = 3.
- **Selection.** Selection *on the simulator's prediction* (advertisers test channels that look promising) leaves (α, β, ω²) unbiased (E8: 0.002/1.001/0.249 vs truth 0/1/0.25) — OLS is conditional on the regressor. Selection *on the outcome* is fatal: keeping only positive results gives α = 0.71, β = 0.56, ω̂² = −0.08 (the simulator looks better than perfect); keeping only |t| > 1.64 gives β = 1.33, ω̂² = 0.50 (2× too pessimistic). The library must ingest every pre-registered experiment including nulls.
- **Sequential certification.** Certifying at every new experiment with the fixed-E UCB has a false-certification rate of 0.28 at the bar (E10, looks at every E from 8 to 150; 0.16 when ω² is 20% above the bar). A prequential e-process — standardised one-step-ahead residuals u_E = (τ̂_E − x_E′β̂_{E−1})/√(1+h_E), which are i.i.d. N(0, ω²+v̄) under the Gaussian model, feeding a likelihood ratio at the bar vs a design alternative ρ = 0.5, certify when LR ≥ 20 — holds the false rate at 0.033/0.009 with power 0.86 (median E 67) at ρ = 0.5; five geometric looks at a = 0.01 each do about as well (0.037/0.013; 0.86 at median E 80). This is C4's confidence-sequence problem in its simplest instance.

## 4. Attack-and-refine history

**Round 1** built §3.1–3.3 with a normal-approximation certification law E ≈ 2 + 2(z_a+z_b)²(ρ+ν)²/(1−ρ)², derived the "match the library precision to the simulator's error" rule ν* = ρ from it, took the quadratic bar as *the* bar, and used the chi-square UCB throughout.

**Round 2 (attack)** broke three things. (i) The certification law under-predicted E by 1.5–3× (power at the predicted E was 0.23–0.76 instead of 0.80): approximating the chi-square *quantile in the denominator* of the UCB by a mean-minus-z·sd is wrong; the exact event is a variance-ratio test with power P(χ² ≤ κχ²_a) and the correct normal approximation carries κz_a, not z_a. Round 3's law (§3.1) matches Monte Carlo in all 12 cells, and the precision rule moved from ν* = ρ to ν* ≈ 0.85 and flat — "match the bar", not "match the simulator". (ii) The quadratic bar is decision-agnostic; for go/no-go decisions its error runs from +250% to −100% depending on how far the prediction sits from the threshold (E3c). Round 3 replaced it with the per-decision closed form (§3.2), which E12b shows recovers most of the oracle's savings where the global bar recovers none. (iii) Heavy tails and heteroscedastic v break the chi-square UCB: t₃ recalibration errors inflate sd(ω̂²) 2.7× (0.77 vs 0.28 at E = 30) and cut UCB coverage to 0.92; with log-sd 0.8 dispersion in v and v estimated on 20 df, to 0.89 (E9). Round 3 tried a symmetric t-interval on ω̂² with the empirical fourth moment — it *under-covered even under normality* (0.875 at E = 30; the statistic is right-skewed) — and then a Satterthwaite-matched chi-square (effective df from the empirical fourth moment, capped at E−2), which recovers about half the shortfall (0.93/0.93 at E = 30, 0.92/0.91 at E = 60) at the price of a 15–50% wider UCB (E13a). The heavy-tail gap is therefore only partly closed; a sub-Gaussian or trimmed variant remains open (BL). Attacks that did *not* land: selection on the simulator's prediction (harmless, E8), estimated v_e at 20 df (coverage 0.948, E9), and seed sensitivity (E1 headline stable to ±0.006 across seeds).

**Round 3 (refine)** also added the strategic-evasion counterexample to the noise-floor test and answered it with the re-estimation test (§3.3), and the sequential-certification failure (0.28 false rate) answered by the prequential e-process (§3.5). **Round 4** re-attacked the e-process (its residuals are exactly independent only under Gaussianity and homoscedasticity; under the E9 stress it will be anti-conservative — untested) and the per-decision bar (it uses V with ω̂² plugged in; when ω̂² < 0 it clips to 0, which is optimistic at small E) and found no further revision that changed a headline number; the certification-drag experiment (E13b) quantifies what the harness itself costs: at ω² = 0.2 against a bar of 0.558, the probability of certifying rises 0.10/0.17/0.27/0.46/0.74 at E = 10/20/40/80/160 (the law says E_exact = 190 for ρ = 0.36, ν = 1.79), and the loss of the bar rule exceeds the oracle's by 0.020–0.060 per decision — the price of not yet knowing the simulator is good.

## 6. The harness (derived specification; not fielded)

1. **Library construction.** Every pre-registered experiment enters, including nulls (E8). Target precision v_e ≈ ω*² — the bar you intend to certify (E2). Record raw data so a second estimator τ̂′ with corr(ε, ε′) as low as possible can be computed (E6).
2. **Pre-registration of predictions.** The simulator's s_e is hashed and time-stamped before readout; the temporal split (pre-/post-training-cutoff) is logged.
3. **Recalibration family** chosen by the failure mode the library reveals: additive only for in-hull use; latent-scale for level-collapse; mixture for sensitivity heterogeneity (E5). The certificate carries the hull (dose, baseline, novelty) it is valid in (E7).
4. **Certificate.** Report ω̂², its chi-square (or Satterthwaite) UCB, and the experiment-equivalents EE = v_x/UCB. Certify against ω*² = γc + √(γ²c² + 2γc·v_x) for continuous decisions, and emit the per-decision verdict σ_pp ψ(|m−τ₀|/σ_pp) ≶ c for threshold decisions.
5. **Contamination gate.** Noise-floor test (necessary), re-estimation test (robust to noise-adding), temporal-split comparison of ω̂² (the only test that catches a memoriser whose training data include *all* library outcomes).
6. **Sequential updating.** Prequential e-process at the bar; re-certify on a kernel-local basis when new categories or doses enter.

## 7. Limitations and failure modes; devil's advocate

*Against the certification law:* it is derived under homoscedastic Gaussian recalibration errors; heavy tails inflate the required E (the sd of ω̂² grows with the fourth moment) and the Satterthwaite fix only half-closes the coverage gap. If v_e are under-reported the estimator over-states ω² (conservative); if over-reported — a real risk with optimistic geo-test analyses — it under-states it and can certify a bad simulator; the bias is exactly −(v_reported − v_true), so a 20% se over-statement at ν = 1 shifts ω̂² by −0.44ω*². Shared shocks across library experiments (same period, same advertiser) are absorbed into ω̂² (conservative) unless the simulator was itself conditioned on that period. *Against the bar:* it is a scalar-decision bar; multichannel budget-coupled decisions need dive 09's ellipsoid, and the value of an experiment also includes its role as trap insurance (dive 05), which the EVSI omits — so the bar is *lenient* toward the simulator. *Against the contamination tests:* a memoriser trained on the full library (no post-cutoff experiments) is invisible to both the floor and re-estimation tests if it memorises the internal re-estimates too; only new experiments catch it, which returns the harness to running experiments. *Against the recalibration result:* the probit-normal sub-model is one heterogeneity structure; the exactness of latent-scale recalibration is specific to it (a logit-with-normal truth would leave a small residual), and the mixture family's success assumes the simulator's persona set spans the responsive segment at all. *Against the whole program:* nothing here says an LLM agent simulator will reach any particular ω; the field numbers (sign agreement 0.70, r = 0.27 on field experiments) suggest ρ ≫ 1 for in-market lift today, in which case the law says the library will correctly *refuse* to certify — a useful output, but not the one E9 hopes for.

## 8. Ledger of headline numbers (with uncertainty)

Certification law E_exact = 157 at (ρ, ν) = (0.5, 1) (MC 157 ± 2); 82 at (0.5, 0.5) (MC 81; clean-room 82); library-precision optimum ν* ≈ 0.85 with cost flat within 1% of ν = 1 and 2.6–2.8× at ν = 0.1. Bar: ω*²/v_x = 0.37 at c̃ = 0.1 (closed form; EVSI MC within 0.8%). Per-decision rule: loss 0.0831 ± 0.001 vs global-bar 0.1122 and oracle 0.0805 at ω² = 0.5 (20,000 decisions). Noise-floor blind spot λ < ω²/(ω²+v): rejection 0.09 ± 0.005 at the boundary for ω = 0.5. Re-estimation test power 0.71 ± 0.007 against the noise-adding memoriser (r = 0.5, E = 30). Recalibration transport: additive −15.7%/+14.5%, latent 0.0% (q = 1); latent −12.7% (q = 0.4, clean-room −13.0%). Sequential: naive false certification 0.284 ± 0.010 vs e-process 0.033 ± 0.004.

5. **Structural family discovery:** replace the hand-chosen mixture by a small library of link-scale families with a Q_min-style selection rule (BL39's machinery), and test whether the family chosen on the library transports to a held-out dose/baseline shell.
6. **Join to C4:** the prequential e-process here is the special case of a confidence sequence for a scale parameter; the adaptive-budget version (spend depends on interim ω̂²) is C4's problem.
