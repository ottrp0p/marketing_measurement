## 1. Problem statement

M7 asks for valid confidence sequences for ROAS "under endogenous, estimator-dependent adaptive designs": the advertiser reads an interim MMM/attribution estimate, moves budget in response, and the next data arrive from the moved budget. Hadad et al. solve inference under a *known* assignment rule; the marketing loop's rule depends on past *estimators*, is usually unlogged, and is often a threshold rule ("scale when the lift is significant"). The catalog frames the open problem as one of validity. This dive shows that framing is mostly wrong: with a policy-agnostic anytime-valid confidence sequence available (§3.1), the naive interval's *validity* is threatened only at second order by smooth feedback and at first order only by a specific drift-plus-trigger combination (§3.2, §3.4), while the loop's real pathology is that a certainty-equivalent allocator **stops learning** — spend converges to an uninformative level, the information matrix stops growing, and the model's reported marginal ROAS at current spend becomes an exact echo of the target that set the spend (§3.3).

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Outcome.** Weekly y_t = α + β x_t + ε_t (linear, mROAS = β) or y_t = α + β H(x_t; K, s) + ε_t with Hill H = x^s/(x^s + K^s) (mROAS(x) = β H′(x)); ε_t independent, mean zero, variance σ², third moment κ₃ (A1: independence across weeks is the assumption the confidence sequence needs; AR(1) and heteroskedastic violations are tested in E9).
- **Estimator.** OLS with intercept on all data through t (linear), or profile-OLS over a K grid (Hill), refreshed every R weeks (A2).
- **Policy (predictable).** x_t = π(θ̂_{t−1}, x_{t−1}) + ξ_t with ξ_t ~ N(0, v) exogenous *exploration* (budget noise not caused by the estimate). Three families: **smooth** x_t = x₀ + g(β̂_{t−1} − β_ref) + ξ_t — the profit-maximizing spend of a linear-quadratic advertiser (profit βx − x²/(2g) − mx, so x* = g(β − m), loop gain g = 1/curvature); **threshold**: jump to x_hi/x_lo when β̂ (deadband) or the naive CI (CI-triggered) crosses the breakeven β_ref, else hold — with the held level either the last *realized* spend (inherits ξ; a random walk) or the last *base* level; **self-driven**: smooth with v = 0. Hill loop: x_t = x*(β̂, K̂) = argmax β̂ Ĥ(x) − x, refreshed every R weeks, partial adjustment ρ. Burn-in T₀ = 8 weeks at a fixed ±20–30% pulse.
- **Targets.** (i) Fixed-time coverage of the naive 95% t/profile-LR interval at T; (ii) time-uniform coverage (the interval covers at *every* weekly look — the guarantee a "scale when significant" protocol actually consumes); (iii) growth of the design information D_T = Σ(x_t − x̄_T)² (linear) or D_H = Σ(H_t − H̄)² at the profiled K (Hill); (iv) the profit cost of any exploration.

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run; 27 searches). Validity under arbitrary predictable designs is *not* open for known noise scale: Abbasi-Yadkori–Pál–Szepesvári (2011) give the self-normalized ellipsoid ‖θ̂_t − θ‖_{V̄_t} ≤ R√(2 log(det(V̄_t)^{1/2} det(λI)^{−1/2}/δ)) + √λ S uniformly in t for any F_{t−1}-measurable design, and Lai & Wei (1982) give strong consistency of LS under adaptive regressors iff λ_min → ∞ and log λ_max = o(λ_min), with asymptotic normality needing the much stronger *stability* condition (the empirical design covariance converges to a deterministic limit). Lindon–Ham–Tingley–Bojinov (JASA 2026) build sequential t/F confidence sequences with unknown variance, but the theorem is stated for i.i.d./fixed designs and their asymptotic version assumes Lai–Wei stability — exactly what a spend-follows-estimate loop can violate; Lai (1976) and Wang–Ramdas (2025) cover the Gaussian mean with unknown variance. The bias literature (Nie–Tian–Taylor–Zou 2018; Shin–Ramdas–Rinaldo 2019/21; Deshpande et al. 2018; Zhang–Janson–Murphy 2020/21; Hadad et al. 2021; Khamaru et al. 2025; Khamaru–Zhang 2024; Kalvit–Zeevi 2021) is about discrete arm choice: optimistic sampling gives negative bias, and non-normality arises when allocation probabilities fail to concentrate. The learning-stall phenomenon is fully established in dynamic pricing: Keskin & Zeevi (2014) show greedy iterated-least-squares pricing converges with positive probability to an "uninformative price" where all demand curves consistent with the data intersect, regret becomes linear, and a price-dispersion condition Σ(p_s − p̄)² ≥ κ√t restores O(√T log T) regret; Harrison–Keskin–Zeevi (2012), den Boer & Zwart (2014, controlled variance pricing), Lai & Wei (1987, probing inputs in self-tuning regulators) and Ljung et al. (1977, closed-loop identifiability) are the same result in other clothes; Ao–Jiang–Simchi-Levi (2026) size exploration for *CI width* in a pricing loop. Marketing: Heusch (arXiv 2608.21130) is a benchmark generator with "algorithmic performance chasing" spend, not an inference method; no published application of confidence sequences to ROAS/MMM exists. The subagent judged unclaimed: (a) naive-CI failure as a function of loop gain, (b) smooth-vs-threshold policies as a determinant of first- vs second-order distortion, (c) exploration sized for inference in the spend loop, (d) anytime-valid sets for marginal ROAS in a saturating response under adaptive spend. Those four are worked below; (c) and (d) turn out to be assemblies of known parts whose value is quantitative, and the *transport* of Keskin–Zeevi to MMM (with the policy-echo corollary) is, to our knowledge, new to the marketing literature.

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — the policy-agnostic anytime-valid confidence sequence as an RSS contour (assembly of known parts; established here that it is valid under estimator-dependent designs with unknown σ, and priced)

For any *proper* mixing prior Π on (θ, σ²) and the true (θ₀, σ₀²), the ratio M_t = ∫ p(y_{1:t} | x_{1:t}, θ, σ²) dΠ / p(y_{1:t} | x_{1:t}, θ₀, σ₀²) is a nonnegative martingale with M₀ = 1 for *every* predictable design — the likelihood factorizes as Π_s p(y_s | x_s, θ, σ²) with x_s any function of the past, including past estimators and past intervals — so Ville gives P(∃t: M_t ≥ 1/δ) ≤ δ. Project the joint set {M_t < 1/δ} onto θ by taking the sup over σ² in the denominator (sup_σ p(y|θ,σ) = (2πRSS_t(θ)/t)^{−t/2}e^{−t/2}), and the set becomes

  **CS_t = {θ : RSS_t(θ) ≤ R*_t}, R*_t = (t/2π)·exp(−2(log m_t + log δ)/t − 1),**

with m_t the normal-inverse-gamma marginal likelihood in closed form (E8 uses θ | σ² ~ N(μ₀, σ²Λ₀⁻¹), σ² ~ IG(a₀, b₀)). The set has *exactly the shape* of the classical likelihood-ratio set — an ellipsoid (θ − θ̂)′X′X(θ − θ̂) ≤ R* − RSS_min around OLS in the linear case, and the same RSS contour in the Hill case with a discrete uniform prior on the K grid mixed with NIG on (α, β) — with a data-dependent radius. The projected β half-width is se_β·ρ_t with ρ_t² = (R*_t − RSS_min)/s², and expanding the exponential gives the radius law

  **ρ_t² ≈ (t−2)·(exp(2(Δ_t + log 1/δ)/t) − 1) ≈ 2 log(1/δ) + 2Δ_t,  Δ_t = log p(y|θ̂,σ̂) − log m_t** (the Occam penalty ≈ ½ log det(I + Λ₀⁻¹X′X) + the σ-prior term + the prior-mismatch term),

versus 1.96² = 3.84 for the naive interval. E8 (g = 3, exploration v = 1): ρ² = 38.1/33.5/32.4/32.8/34.0/35.5 at T = 26–832, i.e. a half-width **2.9–3.0× naive** under a vague prior (Λ₀ = 0.01 I) with time-uniform coverage 0.9993–1.000 (the mixture is conservative in finite time). **Refinement (CS-B):** take the burn-in posterior under a vague prior as the mixing prior for the adaptive phase (legitimate: it is fixed before any adaptive week, and the burn-in design is non-adaptive); the ratio drops to **2.34–2.48× naive** at T = 104–832 versus 1.78–2.05× for Bonferroni over weekly looks, which needs a fixed horizon and a non-adaptive design. Validity holds at every policy tried (E9, T = 104, uniform coverage): smooth g = 10 0.9992, g = 100 with v = 0.09 1.000, self-driven 1.000, CI-triggered 0.9980, deadband 0.9988; and it degrades gracefully under the misspecifications it does not assume: AR(1) 0.3 → 0.9982, AR(1) 0.6 → 0.9778 (naive fixed-time 0.826), heteroskedastic (sd ∝ 1 ± spend deviation) → 0.9985. Power (E10, g = 10, v = 1): CS-B excludes β + 0.4 (a 20% error) in 68% of runs, median week 79, and β + 0.8 always, median week 24; the naive interval excludes the *true* value in 55% of runs by week 33. In the Hill loop the same construction gives time-uniform coverage 1.000 for mROAS at current spend (E12) at 2.3–2.6× the naive profile-LR half-width.

What is and is not new here: the martingale argument is Robbins/Lai; the RSS-contour form and the observation that it needs *no knowledge of the policy* — not even whether the policy exists — are the useful assembly; the price (√(2 log 1/δ + 2Δ) vs 1.96, Δ growing like the log-determinant) is the known price of adaptivity + time-uniformity (Khamaru et al.'s lower bound says the √(d log T) cannot be avoided for general adaptive designs).

### 3.2 Construction 2 — the first-order benignity theorem for smooth feedback (derived here; clean-room verified to three digits)

Write x_t = ξ_t + g d_t with ξ the exogenous design (burn-in pulse + exploration) and d_t = β̂_{t−1} − β_ref the estimator the policy reacts to; expand the OLS error N/D (N = Σ(x_t − x̄)ε_t, D = Σ(x_t − x̄)²) to first order in g: N = N₀ + gN₁, D = D₀ + gD₁ with N₁ = Σ(d_t − d̄)ε_t and D₁ = 2Σ(ξ_t − ξ̄)(d_t − d̄), d_t = Σ_{u<t} w_{u,t} ε_u, w_{u,t} = (ξ_u − ξ̄_{t−1})/S_{t−1}. Conditional on ξ:

- **Bias:** E[N₁] = Σ_t E[d_t ε_t] − E[d̄ Σε_t] = 0 − (σ²/T)Σ_t Σ_{u<t} w_{u,t} = 0 because Σ_{u<t} w_{u,t} = 0 (centred weights); E[N₀D₁] = 2σ² Σ_t (ξ_t − ξ̄)·[Σ_{u<t}(ξ_u − ξ̄)(ξ_u − ξ̄_{t−1})/S_{t−1}] = 2σ² Σ_t (ξ_t − ξ̄)·1 = 0. **The O(g) bias vanishes for any independent mean-zero noise, skewed or not.** Bias is O(g²).
- **Variance:** dVar/dg|₀ = 2[E(N₀N₁)/D₀² − E(N₀²D₁)/D₀³] with E(N₀N₁) = −(κ₃/T)Σ_s (ξ_s − ξ̄)Σ_{u>s} w_{s,u} and E(N₀²D₁) = 2κ₃ Σ_t (ξ_t − ξ̄)² Σ_{s>t} (ξ_s − ξ̄) w_{t,s}: **the O(g) variance term is proportional to the third moment κ₃ and vanishes for symmetric noise.** Its sign is −sign(κ₃·g): positive skew with positive gain *shrinks* the true variance below the naive se² (conservative interval); negative skew (or a policy that cuts spend when the estimate rises) inflates it.

E2 (common random numbers, T = 40, v = 1, R = 40,000): Gaussian noise dbias = +0.00001…+0.00007 at g = 0.5…4 (sd(β̂) = 0.15), dVar/g² = −0.00060/−0.00055/−0.00046/−0.00030 (quadratic); skew 2 (κ₃ = 2): dVar/g = −0.00186/−0.00198/−0.00204/−0.00189 (linear) versus the formula evaluated on the realized exploration designs **−0.00156**, which matches −0.00186 + 0.00030 (the g² term at g = 0.5) exactly. Clean-room: −0.00181/−0.00195/−0.00203 (+skew), +0.00118/+0.00079/+0.00013 (−skew), dVar/g² = −0.00064/−0.00056 (Gaussian). Consequences at T = 104 (E1, E3): with v = 1 the naive fixed-time coverage is 0.950–0.957 for g = 0…100 (bias ≤ 0.012 = 0.24 sd at g = 100, where the clip at 5% of x₀ binds); skew +2 pushes coverage *up* to 0.968/0.978 at g = 10/30, skew −2 *down* to 0.931/0.920, and to 0.924 at g = 100 with v = 0.09 (E-attack). This is the quantitative content of "loop gain": a smooth allocator is second-order for symmetric noise and its first-order term has a known sign and size under skew.

### 3.3 Construction 3 — the learning stall and the policy echo (Keskin–Zeevi transported to spend; established here by simulation; manifold identity exact)

Self-driven loop (v = 0), g = 10 (E5): the design information **freezes** — median D_T = 22.9/23.8/23.9/23.9 at T = 30/104/416/832 — and with it everything else: sd(β̂_T) = 0.106/0.099/0.098/0.098, mean naive se 0.217 at every T, naive half-width 0.425 at T = 30 and at T = 832, CS half-width 1.19–1.22. The spend converges (mean |x_T − x_{T−1}| = 3·10⁻⁵ at T = 832) to a level x_∞ at which the fitted line crosses the true line, so further observations there confirm the fit and never move the slope: **the stall manifold** (α̂ − α) = −(β̂ − β)x_∞, verified as mean |(α̂ − α) + (β̂ − β)x_∞| = 0.028 against the unconstrained scale |β̂ − β|x₀ = 0.375 (clean-room 0.027 vs 0.371), with sd(x_∞) = g·sd(β̂_∞) exactly (0.9799 = 0.9799). This is Keskin–Zeevi's uninformative price with spend in place of price and slope in place of demand sensitivity. Two things the pricing literature does not emphasize follow: (i) the naive interval is **conservative and frozen** (fixed-time coverage 0.96–0.98 across g = 1…100), because the loop's negative feedback corrects the estimate (sd(β̂) < se) without adding dispersion — the stall is invisible to a coverage audit and visible only as an interval that stops shrinking; (ii) **gain trades slope error for spend error one-for-one**: sd(β̂_∞) = 0.290/0.186/0.097/0.058/0.041 and sd(x_∞) = 0.29/0.56/0.97/1.74/2.94 at g = 1/3/10/30/100, with the per-period profit loss g·Var(β̂_∞)/2 nearly flat (0.042–0.083, i.e. ≈ 1% of the LQ profit at x₀ = 5). A one-shot heuristic (the frozen line passes through the true point at x_∞ and the burn-in cloud: n₀g²e³ + (D₀ + gB)e = A with A ~ N(0, σ²D₀), B ~ N(0, n₀σ²)) reproduces the order of magnitude (sd 0.317/0.238/0.134/0.070/0.034) but not the constants — the transient wandering before the freeze buys identification at low gain and the clip distorts it at high gain; it is reported as a partial failure, not a law.

**Hill loop (E12, E13):** the realistic MMM loop — β = 40, K = 5, s = 1, σ = 2, refit every 4 weeks, spend set to the fitted optimum — stalls identically: sd of the mROAS error at current spend 0.179/0.164/0.158/0.155 at T = 52/104/416/832, D_H plateau 0.047, naive half-width 0.44 → 0.38 (≈ 40% of mROAS) forever, fixed-time coverage 0.975–0.979, and the stall survives every realism knob: refresh 1 or 13 weeks, partial adjustment ρ = 0.5/0.25, σ = 1/4, s = 2, half or double gain (sd_m at T = 416 within 5% of T = 104 in all nine configurations). The stall loss is small — 0.08–0.10 per week ≈ 0.5% of profit — because the profit curve is flat at its optimum. **The policy echo:** at the stall the model's own marginal ROAS at current spend is β̂K̂/(x_T + K̂)² = **1.0000 ± 0.005** (clean-room: exactly 1.0000) — it is the breakeven target that set the spend, returned as a "measurement" — while the true mROAS at that spend has sd 0.16 with 5–95% range [0.81, 1.32], and the curve parameters wander freely along the manifold (β̂ 5–95% [35, 220] against 40; K̂ [0.5, 47] against 5; 37% of runs pin K̂ at a grid edge). A dashboard reading "mROAS ≈ 1.0 at current spend, stable for two years" is, in this regime, not evidence about the channel; it is the loop closing on itself. This gives E2's "measuring a learning system" a precise statistical content: the estimand the loop reports converges to the policy's own set-point.

**Restoring learning (E6, E12):** Keskin–Zeevi's dispersion condition transports unchanged. Exploration ξ_t ~ N(0, t^{−2d}): d = 0.25 (Σξ² ∝ √t) keeps D growing (57.6 → 163 from T = 104 to 832; sd(β̂) 0.105 → 0.071), d = 0.5 (Σξ² ∝ log t) barely (36.9 → 66; 0.108 → 0.095), d = 1 not at all (27 → 32). In the Hill loop, decaying exploration c = 2·t^{−1/4} (≈ 20% of spend in week 8, 6% by week 832) is *profitable*: cumulative loss 60.4 ± 1.4 vs 82.6 ± 3.1 for the stalled loop at T = 832, with the mROAS sd cut from 0.157 to 0.092 — while constant exploration at 1.0 or 1.5 (11–16% of spend) loses more than the stall (95.8, 162.7). Exploration that is worth it for the loop's own profit is small and shrinking; exploration for *inference* has to be justified by information value outside the loop (dive 03's EVSI), which is the pricing question next.

### 3.4 Construction 4 — the failure taxonomy and the profit price of a valid interval (derived and verified here; strong-feedback regime qualified)

**Taxonomy (E1, E4, E7).** Three sources of naive-interval failure in the loop, ordered by size at T = 104 with weekly looks: *peeking* (time-uniform coverage 0.66 for a fixed design, 0.67–0.69 under smooth feedback, 0.55 under CI-triggering; 0.40–0.56 by T = 416) ≫ *drift × trigger* (fixed-time coverage 0.902/0.881/0.855 at T = 104/208/416 for the CI-triggered policy whose hold level is the last realized spend, se under-estimating the true sd by 35–96%; deadband RW-hold 0.915/0.909/0.929) ≫ *smooth adaptivity* (0.950–0.957, second-order, §3.2). Two controls locate the middle term: a pure random-walk spend with no adaptivity covers at 0.943–0.946 (Lai–Wei consistency without stability is enough at these T), and the same trigger rules with a *fixed* hold level cover at 0.946–0.967 — the anti-conservatism needs both a drifting design and estimate-triggered jumps (the jump's lever arm is selected on the estimate's error, and the drift lets that selection dominate the design as T grows; the clean-room agent flagged this distinction independently). In the stalled loop, peeking itself becomes nearly harmless (0.77 uniform coverage at T = 104 vs 0.66 fixed-design) because the looks stop being informative. So the M7 threat as posed — "estimator-dependent adaptivity invalidates the interval" — is real only for the drift-plus-trigger policy, and the CS-B removes all three at once (0.998–1.000 in every cell).

**Pricing law.** In the LQ model a width-w interval needs D_T ≥ ρ²σ²/w² (ρ = 1.96 naive, ρ_t for the CS); exogenous exploration of variance v costs v/(2g) profit per week and buys D ≈ Tv, so the total cost of the interval is

  **C(w) = ρ²σ²/(2g w²), independent of the horizon** — the price of inference is paid once, in profit, and only its *timing* depends on T.

E11 (w = 0.15, σ = 1, 40-point log grid on v, ±14% resolution): weak feedback g = 1, theory 85.3, measured exogenous cost 73.2/76.3/91.4/89.2 at T = 52/104/208/416 (horizon-free within grid resolution; total deviation cost 72–93). Strong feedback g = 10, theory 8.5: exogenous cost falls with T (7.3/6.3/5.3/4.3) because the loop's own wobble supplies dispersion (median D 175–191 from Tv of only 47–146), but the *total* deviation cost rises (9.0/10.1/12.3/17.2): the loop's persistent offset from x* costs profit without adding dispersion. **Offset costs, dispersion informs** — the loop's self-exploration is not free information, it is information bought with the estimation regret the advertiser pays anyway, and at high gain it overpays.

## 4. Attack-and-refine history

**Round 1 (construct).** Built the linear closed loop with a smooth profit-max policy, the vague-prior NIG mixture CS, and the self-driven variant. Immediate findings: smooth feedback did nothing to fixed-time coverage; the CS was valid but 2.9× naive; the self-driven loop had *conservative* coverage. Predicted, from the self-consistent recursion D′ = g²σ²/D, that self-driven information grows like gσ√(2t) (a T^{−1/4} learning rate).

**Round 2 (attack).** (a) The √t prediction was refuted outright: D freezes (23.8 → 23.9 from T = 104 to 832). Diagnosis: a frozen slope error contributes an *offset*, which the sample mean absorbs, not dispersion — the recursion assumed fresh errors every week. The stall manifold identity replaced the growth law and was verified to 0.028 vs 0.375. (b) Attempted a closed-form stall law (the cubic); it reproduces the scaling but misses the constants by up to 40%; reported as a failure. (c) The first-order expansion was attacked with skewed noise: the O(g) *bias* term survived the attack (exactly zero, as derived), the O(g) *variance* term appeared with the predicted sign and size (E2). (d) The threshold-policy result (coverage 0.90) was attacked by the clean-room agent, who noticed it depends on the hold branch inheriting exploration noise. Controls confirmed: pure random walk 0.946, fixed-hold trigger 0.963–0.967 — the first-order effect is drift × trigger, not thresholding per se. (e) The CS was attacked with AR(1) and heteroskedastic errors: 0.978 at AR(1) = 0.6 (naive 0.826) — degraded but not broken. (f) The first burn-in implementation (no pulse, estimate undefined) produced a spurious first-week collapse of spend; replaced with a pulsed burn-in before any number was kept. (g) The g = 100/v = 0.09 CS-B mean width was dominated by a handful of clipped runs (mean 2·10⁶); medians reported.

**Round 3 (refine).** (a) CS-B (burn-in posterior as mixing prior): 2.9× → 2.3× naive, validity retained in every cell. (b) The Hill loop was added as a second independent construction of the stall, then stressed across nine realism configurations; the policy-echo identity emerged from asking what the stalled model *reports* rather than what it estimates. (c) The Keskin–Zeevi dispersion condition was tested as an exploration schedule in both loops, and the profit comparison (decaying exploration beats the stall by 27%, constant exploration loses to it) replaced the naive "explore to learn" recommendation. (d) The pricing law was derived and tested at two gains; the strong-gain qualification ("offset costs, dispersion informs") came from the discrepancy between exogenous and total cost.

**Round 4 (attack again).** Seed sensitivity: CI-triggered fixed-time coverage 0.904–0.912 over five seeds of 4,000; stall D median 23.6–24.7 over five seeds; the smooth g = 10 coverage is 0.943 (seed 1, R = 4,000) vs 0.954–0.957 (R = 8,000 and clean-room three seeds of 40,000) — inside the stated 0.94–0.97 band, reported as the band. Negative-skew × low-exploration × high gain was the worst smooth case found (0.920–0.924). No material revision from this round; the two-consecutive-rounds criterion is met.

## 6. Limitations and failure modes; devil's advocate

*Against "smooth feedback is second-order":* the theorem is conditional on a persistent exogenous design; as v → 0 the expansion parameter is g·sd(β̂)/√v and the O(g²) terms are not small — at g = 100 with v = 0.09 the bias reaches 0.24 sd and coverage 0.92 under negative skew. It also assumes independent errors; under AR(1) = 0.6 the *fixed-design* naive interval already fails (0.83), so the loop is not the binding problem there. *Against the stall:* real spend is not the fitted optimum — finance cuts, seasonality-driven budgets and channel caps are exploration in disguise, and any of them re-opens learning; the stall is the limit of a *disciplined* certainty-equivalent allocator, which is exactly what automated MMM-to-budget pipelines (E6 in dive 05, "Learning to Spend") aim to build. Drift in the true β does not rescue it: the loop still observes at one spend level and the slope stays unidentified while the fitted value tracks. *Against the policy echo:* it is exact only when spend sits at the fitted optimum; with a fixed ROAS target ≠ 1 or a budget constraint the echo is of that target instead, and with lagged refits it is approximate (sd 0.005). *Against the CS:* 2.3–3× naive is a heavy price at T ≈ 100; the Bonferroni comparison (1.8×) is not a fair competitor (fixed horizon, non-adaptive design), but a practitioner who never peeks and uses a smooth policy is entitled to the naive interval, by §3.2. Ville's conservatism (coverage 0.998 at δ = 0.05) means a calibrated δ could be larger; we did not tune it. *Against the pricing law:* it is exact only in the LQ model with exogenous exploration; the Hill loop's profit curvature at x* sets g, and the law's strong-feedback failure (total cost 17 vs 8.5 at T = 416) shows the loop's own regret cannot be booked as inference spend. *Against novelty:* every ingredient is known (Robbins mixtures, Lai–Wei, Keskin–Zeevi, Nie et al.); the dive's contribution is the transport, the taxonomy with sizes, the first-order coefficient, and the echo corollary. *Against the simulation ranges:* one channel, no adstock, no confounders, weekly data, T ≤ 832; the Hill K grid caps at 60 and pins 37% of stalled runs at an edge.

## 8. Next steps for a future session

1. **Calibrated-δ mixture:** Ville's slack leaves the CS at coverage 0.998 for δ = 0.05; a stitched or predictable-mixture boundary tuned to the burn-in posterior (Howard et al.'s style) could take the price from 2.3× toward the 1.8× Bonferroni figure while staying policy-agnostic — a direct width win.
2. **Stall detection as a monitoring statistic:** the loop's pathology is invisible to coverage but visible in D_t's growth; derive the null distribution of ΔD_t/D_t under a stalled vs learning loop and add "information clock stopped" to dive 08's deadband protocol as a trigger to inject exploration (dive 05's coverage constraint, now with a statistic).
3. **The echo as a test:** if the reported mROAS at current spend equals the target to within its own se for k consecutive refreshes, that is evidence of a stalled loop; compute its false-alarm rate against a genuinely breakeven channel.
4. **Multichannel stall geometry:** with n channels the uninformative set is an (n−1)-manifold and budget coupling (dive 09) constrains where the loop freezes; find which channel combinations the loop can never learn and whether dive 04's switch experiments are the cheapest escape.
5. **Adstock and confounders:** the stall with carryover (the fitted value at x_∞ must match a *distributed* lag) and with a demand confounder that also drives spend (dive 11's leakage) — does the echo still hold, and does the CS's independence assumption survive weekly autocorrelation of size found in real MMM residuals?
6. **Join to dive 15:** the prequential e-process there is the scale-parameter special case of §3.1; when spend depends on the interim ω̂², the certification law needs the same RSS-contour radius.
7. **Empirical:** apply the stall diagnostics (D_t growth, echo test) to Heusch's endogenous-spend benchmark, whose "algorithmic performance chasing" generator is the self-driven loop of §3.3.
