# Dive 07 — Refresh stability and analyst degrees of freedom (B1: barriers M6/M4)

**Question.** Why do MMM outputs flip between refreshes, how fast should they flip, and can a refresh scheme be *stable without lying*? Deliverables: a quantitative flip-rate law, a diagnosis of what actually drives refresh instability in nonlinear MMMs, an overlap-corrected sequential updating scheme (the double-counting problem Meridian names but doesn't solve), a characterization of when analyst degrees of freedom amplify instability, and a pre-registrable reporting protocol.

**Status of the field (literature subagent, confirmed gaps).** Refresh instability is universally acknowledged — Meridian's docs warn "even a relatively small amount of new data may have a noticeable effect"; Robyn issues #890/#507 document refreshes zeroing out channels; Recast documents the 0.75→0.2 ROAS drift case — but *no one publishes a measured flip rate*, and **no stability-constrained MMM refresh scheme with formal guarantees exists anywhere** (searched vendors, arXiv, Meridian/Robyn/PyMC docs). Meridian states the double-counting problem verbatim ("setting priors that match an old MMM's results effectively counts the old data twice") and leaves it open. Robyn's `robyn_refresh` bounds hyperparameters near the last run — stability by construction, criticized by Recast as circular. Adjacent machinery exists unassembled: power priors (Ibrahim–Chen 2000; closed-form optimal discount a₀\*=d/(2N₀D̄₀+d), arXiv:2608.12159), exponential forgetting (Kulhavý 1984; Smídl–Gustafsson 2012), overlapping-sample statistics (Harri–Brorsen), Sheppard's orthant formula, Type-S errors (Gelman–Carlin 2014), and the vibration-of-effects result that 31% of variables show sign reversal across specifications (Patel–Burford–Ioannidis 2015). Sequential posterior→prior is known to fail under misspecification (Kessler et al. 2023) and correlated datasets (arXiv:2605.17868). This dive assembles and extends these pieces for the MMM refresh problem.

## 1. Setup

Weekly national MMM, J=3 channels: y_t = c₀ + Σⱼ βⱼ·Hill(adstock(x_jt; αⱼ); Kⱼ, sⱼ) + annual seasonality + ε_t, ε iid N(0, σ²), AR(1)-lognormal exogenous spend. Refresh k fits the window of W=104 weeks ending at t_k = W + kΔ; **overlap fraction ω = (W−Δ)/W**. Reported quantity: window ROAS R̂ⱼ; the trust-destroying event is a **ranking flip**: sign(R̂ᵢ−R̂ⱼ) reversing between adjacent refreshes. 15 parameters, MAP with weak priors + Laplace covariance (transformed scale). Base design deliberately MMM-realistic: weakly identified (log-ROAS estimation noise ≈ 0.4–0.9), per dive 01's ridge results.

## 2. Established results (verified here, incl. clean-room)

**T1 — The flip-rate law.** For asymptotically linear estimators (OLS regime), adjacent-refresh estimates correlate at ρ ≈ ω, and the pairwise ranking flip probability with signal z = |true gap|/sd(diff) is

  **F(z, ρ) = 2[Φ(−z) − Φ₂(−z, −z; ρ)]**,  reducing at z=0 to Sheppard's ½ − arcsin(ρ)/π.

Verified: linear probe, Δ ∈ {4,…,104}: ρ_emp within 0.01 of ω; F within 0.005–0.016 of prediction (n=400 reps; clean-room agent re-derived the formula and confirmed at 40k reps, F within 0.005–0.015; its ρ ran 0.837 vs ω=0.875 — finite-sample design variation, see T5).

**T2 — The cadence law.** Annualized null-pair flips = (52/Δ)·F(0, 1−Δ/W) ≈ (52/π)√(2/(WΔ)) ∝ **Δ^(−1/2)**: *more frequent refreshes generate more observed flips per year* — quarterly→weekly multiplies visible ranking churn by ≈√13 ≈ 3.6 in principle (measured Δ=4 vs Δ=26 ratio: 2.26±0.04 emp vs 2.50 exact-law; the asymptote overstates by ~10% at small Δ, and at Δ≤2 the law is conservative — empirical flips run *below* prediction). A CMO buying monthly refreshes is buying more apparent instability at identical model quality; this is arithmetic, not model failure.

**T3 — Naive posterior-as-prior is quantified overconfidence.** The scheme every vendor is tempted by (previous posterior as prior + full new window's likelihood) counts each datapoint once per window containing it: posterior precision grows ∝ (k+1); width collapses like **1/√(k+1)** (exact-conjugate sim: 0.327 vs 1/3 at k=8; clean-room: 0.326–0.328), 90% coverage collapses to 0.52–0.56 (linear) and **0.24 (nonlinear MMM)** at k=8, while flips drop to 0.036–0.048. This is the precise mechanics of "stability by freezing beliefs": Robyn-refresh-style anchoring buys its calm with collapsing honesty. RMSE simultaneously *rises* (0.51→0.66 log-ROAS) — the frozen belief also locks in early-window errors.

**T4 — The corrected scheme (CPP): increment likelihood + power-prior discount a=ω.** Condition each refresh only on the Δ *new* observations (with adstock warmed on the full spend history — resetting the transient inside increments wrecks it, re-confirming dive 01's transient result), with prior = (previous posterior)^a ⊗ (base prior)^(1−a) and **a = ω**, i.e. Gaussian precision blend aP_prev + (1−a)P₀. Derivation: steady state P\* = P₀ + (W/Δ)P_Δ ≈ the honest moving-window precision; the discount that reproduces a W-week window is exactly the overlap fraction — connecting moving-window practice to the forgetting-factor/power-prior literature in one line. Verified exact-conjugate: steady-state width within 5% of the moving-window width, coverage 0.97–0.98 (conservative — clean-room *derived* the conservatism: geometric weights spread information over effective sample ≈ 2W−Δ, so true sd ≈ 0.73× reported; CPP is variance-better than a hard window at equal claimed width, RMSE 0.166 vs 0.233 in the linear probe). In the nonlinear MMM, Laplace propagation still overconcentrates (coverage 0.80); a **variance floor** (per-parameter prior-sd floor ≈ 0.3×base) restores coverage 0.91 at flip rate 0.07–0.08 vs IND's 0.26. Without the floor: coverage 0.59, trap rate 23% — dive 05's low-drift trap paradox reappearing in refresh clothing.

**T5 — What actually causes MMM refresh flips (the dive's sharpest result).** In the nonlinear MMM the naive law fails *informatively*: measured ρ = 0.46–0.63 ≪ ω = 0.875, roughly doubling flip rates. The attack rounds eliminated every folk explanation one by one:
- **Not optimizer churn**: multistart from 10 dispersed inits converges to identical ROAS (sd ≤ 5e-4); MAP is unique.
- **Not warm-start path dependence**: cold starts give *higher* ρ (0.70 vs 0.60).
- **Not the window-dependent ROAS functional**: evaluating ROAS at a fixed reference path leaves ρ unchanged (0.601 vs 0.589).
- **Not SNR**: cutting σ 9× leaves sd(d) unchanged (≈0.38) — flip dispersion is **noise-floor-independent**.
- **Not fixed by pulsed identifying designs alone**: dive-01-style block pulsing restores the correct pseudo-true *sign* (z: 0.22→1.10) but ρ stays ≤ 0.31.

The mechanism, isolated by construction: an exactly-linear-in-y "anchored" estimator (one Gauss–Newton step from a fixed anchor) *still* shows ρ = 0.568 — so the culprit is the **rotation of the ill-conditioned information matrix across windows**. Exact decomposition of the anchored estimator: (i) a σ-independent *design-wobble* term — the prior-shrunk component in the near-null eigenspace, which swings as that eigenspace rotates with each window's realized spend — contributing 21% of variance at ρ_X = 0.41; (ii) the noise term itself correlating at only 0.676 < ω because shared noise enters through rotating projections. Predicted ρ = 0.620 vs measured 0.568. **Refresh instability in weakly identified MMMs is a geometry problem — the identified ridge's thin directions rotate with the spend realization — not a noise, optimizer, or seed problem.** Better data won't fix it; only information in the thin directions (priors carried across refreshes, calibration experiments, or identifying spend perturbation) will. This also explains *why* CPP works: the carried prior fills the rotating null space.

**T6 — Analyst degrees of freedom: the Janus condition.** Three refutation-grade results. (a) Deterministic selection over nested specs (AIC over W×seasonality menu) picks one spec 100% of the time — no churn added. (b) Random tie-breaking among near-tied specs whose pseudo-true answers *agree in sign* (tight short- vs long-carryover camps) *reduced* flips (0.204±0.024 vs 0.279±0.027) — strong camp priors stabilize more than arbitrariness destabilizes. (c) When near-tied specs **disagree in sign** on the reported gap (the documented Robyn Pareto-front situation; Patel–Ioannidis's 31% Janus rate), random selection at 30% switch rate amplifies flips **5.8×** at matched signal (0.295 vs 0.051): flips ≈ π_switch·P(spec sign dominates) + noise floor. So M4 bites *iff the tie set is sign-heterogeneous on the decision quantity* — which is precisely what decomp.RSSD-style multi-objective fronts produce. Pre-registration of the spec has value exactly and only there.

**T7 — The regime duel** (n=40/regime, W=104, Δ=13, 12 refreshes; ±SE ≈ 0.03–0.08; seed sweeps at 2 extra seeds):

| scheme | static: flip / log-RMSE / cover | drift: trap | break: end-error / trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| IND (refit fresh) | 0.28 / 0.89 / 0.72 | 0.60 | 0.83 / 0.55 |
| NPP (naive P-as-prior) | 0.04 / 1.15 / **0.24** | — | 1.18 / 0.70 |
| FRZ (freeze media, refit base) | 0.01 / 0.59 / 0.93 | 0.24±0.03 | 0.58±0.03 / 0.36–0.47 |
| CPPD (CPP + floor + drift test) | 0.12 / 0.48 / 0.91 | **0.10±0.05** | 0.60±0.05 / 0.20–0.44 |

CPPD dominates or ties everywhere honest schemes compete: half IND's flips, best RMSE, calibrated. The interesting honest findings: **freezing is genuinely good in the estimation-noise-dominated regime** (static + mild drift RMSE better than refitting fresh! — because refitting re-rolls the rotation lottery each quarter) — this is *why* practitioners freeze; its cost is structural: breaks become permanent bias behind stale-but-wide intervals, and its stability is semantic fraud (the number isn't an estimate anymore). And on breaks, **CPPD ≈ FRZ on recovery** (0.60 vs 0.58; the seed-7 advantage 0.49 vs 0.66 didn't survive the sweep): at this identification level *no refresh scheme recovers from a break quickly* — 13-week increments barely re-estimate. Breaks are what calibration experiments are for (dive 05's trap-insurance result, rediscovered from the refresh side). The drift test fires at 0.14 well-specified / 0.17 misspecified (nominal 0.01 — inflated by ignoring parameter uncertainty; acceptable but crude).

**T8 — The reporting layer: pre-registered hysteresis.** Report a ranking change only when the new estimate crosses a deadband of h·sd: at h=1, reported flips fall 0.25→0.057 (**−78%**) with wrong-order rate statistically unchanged (0.518→0.518, n=160). For near-null pairs the reported ranking was a coin toss either way; hysteresis doesn't hide information, it stops narrating the coin. Unlike coefficient-freezing this is honest *because it lives at the reporting layer with the rule pre-registered* and the underlying posterior keeps updating.

**Negative/power control.** Linear big-gap pair: flip = 0.007 (10/1400) — the instrument reads ≈0 when identification is strong, so the high nonlinear flip rates are signal, not artifact. No tested *nonlinear* configuration reached this floor — itself a statement about MMM-realistic identification.

## 3. Attack-and-refine history

Round 1 built T1–T4 plus a "churn" story: ρ = ω(1−h) with h the optimizer-idiosyncratic variance share, seemingly supported by a multistart probe (h up to 0.40). Round 2 killed it: the failed power control (σ/3 → flips unchanged) prompted the multistart check that found the MAP unique — the probe's dispersion came from a few multimodal outlier windows. Round 2 also caught the adstock-transient reset inside CPP increments (RMSE 0.71→0.55 after warming on spend history) and a metric confound (with high-cv spend the *true* window-ROAS ordering itself moves; fixed-reference truth separates estimate wobble from truth motion). Round 3 replaced churn with the rotation mechanism via the anchored-estimator construction and its exact decomposition (ρ_pred 0.620 vs 0.568), ran the spec-selection falsifications (T6a/b — both *against* the naive M4 story — then established the Janus condition), and the seed sweeps that demoted the break-recovery claim to a null. Round 4 (drift-regime seed sweep, CPPD-consistency check: flips 0.08–0.12 vs law(z, ρ=0.95)≈0.09 ✓) produced no material revision → stop. Clean-room verification confirmed T1/T3/T4 and forced softening of T2's ratio claim.

## 4. Devil's advocate

*Against T1/T2:* the law's z uses the empirical sd — self-referential as stated; its falsifiable content is the orthant form plus ρ=ω, and ρ=ω holds only in the linear/strong-ID regime the law was derived for. The cadence law counts *visible* flips, and one can argue frequent refreshes each carry less per-flip surprise. *Against T4/T7:* CPPD's floor (0.3) was tuned in-sample here, its drift test is a crude chi-square at a fudged threshold, coverage claims lean on Laplace approximations twice over, and FRZ matches it on break recovery — a frozen model plus a scheduled annual full rebuild might match CPPD in practice at lower complexity (untested). Conservatism (cover 0.97 linear) means CPP over-reports width ~1.4×; skeptics may call that miscalibration too. *Against T5:* the rotation decomposition was computed for the anchored estimator, not the full MAP (gap 0.62 vs 0.57 unexplained ~0.05); "σ-independence" was shown at two σ values; and all of it is within one DGP family — TVC models or MCMC posterior means (vs MAP) could change the geometry (BL19). *Against T6:* the 5.8× Janus amplification is synthetic-normal, with the sign-disagreeing tie engineered; the MMM-fitted version produced camps that agreed — the field's actual tie-sets may sit anywhere between. *Against T8:* hysteresis at h=1 delays true regime-change reporting by construction; the wrong-order metric was flat partly because the pair was near-null — for a well-identified pair a large h would trade real staleness.

## 5. Limitations

Laplace/MAP throughout (no full MCMC — BL13/BL19 unresolved and directly relevant); J=3, one DGP family, known σ; drift test ignores parameter uncertainty; ROAS functional, not budget-decision regret (dives 03/04 machinery would sharpen "flip" into "reallocation flip"); spec menu small; no endogenous spend (BL15).

## 6. The protocol (assembled deliverable for B1)

Pre-register: (1) the spec, frozen, with re-selection only at scheduled annual rebuilds — mandatory when candidate specs disagree in sign (T6c), cheap insurance otherwise; (2) CPPD updating: increment likelihood, a=ω discount, variance floor, drift test with a pre-registered escalation (floor drop / experiment trigger, not silent refit); (3) hysteresis reporting at h≈1 posterior-sd with the deadband published; (4) cadence chosen knowing flips/year ∝ Δ^(−1/2) — and the refresh deck reports F(z,ρ) *expected* flips alongside realized ones, so stakeholders see "3 flips expected, 3 observed" instead of "the model changed its mind again."
