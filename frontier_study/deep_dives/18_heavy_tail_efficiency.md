## 1. Problem statement

M6 asks three things: (i) the semiparametric efficiency bound for the ATE of ad exposure when per-user revenue is zero-inflated and heavy-tailed (Lewis–Rao's regime: sd/mean ≈ 10, a 5% lift is 0.005 sd); (ii) how close CUPED, latent stratification and trimmed/winsorized estimators come to it; (iii) whether there is a principled theory trading *identified* trimming bias against variance to minimize decision regret rather than MSE. The catalog phrases (i) as if a single bound existed. The first result of this dive is that the *unrestricted* bound is Hahn's and it is simply Lewis–Rao's floor: covariates lower it only through their R², and if the outcome variance is infinite it is infinite. Everything better than that comes from a *restriction on how treatment acts on the two margins* of the outcome (incidence and basket), and the dive's contribution is to make that restriction explicit, price it (a closed-form variance-ratio law), show what it costs when wrong (an exact composition-bias law), and build the estimator that keeps most of the gain while dropping the worst of the assumption (the rank-identified basket ratio). Part (iii) turns out to have a short exact answer — for a flat prior and additive bias, regret *is* MSE/2, so the question is not "regret vs MSE" but "which prior and which kind of bias" — and a useful sufficient statistic for the winsorization choice (the retention index).

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Design.** Two arms of n users each (π = ½), user-level randomization, SUTVA, no interference. Outcome Y = B·V with B ∈ {0,1} (bought) and V > 0 (basket), observed once per user.
- **Strata (A1, monotonicity).** Three principal strata as in Berman–Feit: always-buyers (mass p0; B = 1 in both arms), influenced buyers (mass p1 − p0; B = 1 only if treated), never-buyers. No defiers. Write ℓ := p1/p0 − 1 (relative incidence lift; ℓ = 0.05 in the base case), w0 := p0/p1, w1 := 1 − w0 = (p1−p0)/p1 (the influenced share of treated positives; w1 = 0.048 at ℓ = 0.05).
- **Basket laws.** Always-buyers' basket under control has law F_A with mean m_A and log-sd s (base: lognormal, s_log ≈ 1.4 after user heterogeneity, m_A ≈ $389, CV_V ≈ 2.7, so CV_Y ≈ 12.7 — the Lewis–Rao regime; also Pareto with tail index α ∈ (1, 2), infinite variance). Under treatment always-buyers' basket is scaled by (1+γ) (intensive effect; base γ = 0). Influenced buyers' basket has law F_I with mean κ·m_A (composition parameter κ; base κ = 1 means F_I = F_A). Optional tail effect: only the top decile of always-buyers is scaled by (1+g_tail).
- **Targets.** ATE per user τ = (p1 − p0)κ m_A + p0 γ m_A (+ tail term); relative lift L := τ/(p0 m_A) = ℓκ + γ; the go/no-go decision "scale the campaign iff L > L0" with linear loss |L − L0| when wrong.
- **Covariate.** A 26-period pre-period revenue P sharing the user's latent propensity (corr(Y, P) ≈ 0.34, R² ≈ 0.12 in the base; Lewis–Rao report up to 0.40).
- **Estimators.** DIM; CUPED (linear on P); winsorized DIM at a pooled cap c (arm-common, fixed by the pooled positive quantile); EXT — the extensive-only estimator (p̂1 − p̂0)·m̂ with m̂ the pooled positive mean (assumes F_I = F_A, γ = 0); SCALE — m̂0(p̂1(1+γ̂) − p̂0) with γ̂ from a rank (Hodges–Lehmann) or median shift of log V between arms' positives (assumes κ = 1); κ-ISO — EXT with a prior on κ; RANK-κ — the Round-3 estimator of §3.3.

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run, 28 searches). The bound: Hahn 1998 / Hirano–Imbens–Ridder 2003, V_eff = E[σ₁²(X)/e + σ₀²(X)/(1−e) + (τ(X) − τ)²]; knowing the propensity does not lower the ATE bound; with Var(Y) = ∞ no regular √n estimator exists. Lewis & Rao 2015 (QJE): sd/mean median 9.9 across 25 retail experiments, implied R² = 5.4e−6, median ROI CI > 100 pp; lagged sales cut residual variance ≤ 40% (se −23%); a one-sentence remark that the incidence/basket/frequency decomposition "could reduce variance", not developed. Latent stratification: Berman & Feit (Marketing Science 2024; arXiv 1911.08438) — the three-stratum monotone model with lognormal strata fitted as a finite mixture, 30–60% variance reduction on five catalog experiments; parametric, no bound stated. Johnson–Lewis–Reiley "When less is more" (31% precision from dropping demonstrably unaffected sales). CUPED (Deng et al. 2013) and its heavy-tail descendants: Wang & Burke 2019 (winsorization with arm-dependent caps inflates false positives; Huber + orthogonalized pre-period), Wicker 2026 (pooled vs arm-stratified winsorization: bias vs Type I trade; estimates move 53% between conventions), STATE (Student-t likelihood, estimates a robust location — a different estimand), Charette & Boudreault 2025 (CUPED + Yuen trimmed t), Najmi & Keselman 2026 (DML-optimal adjustment, no tails). Trimmed Match (Chen & Au, AoAS 2022): trims *randomization-symmetric residuals*, not the outcome, so it is unbiased exactly under homogeneous iROAS; trim rate by CI-width minimization (Jaeckel). Robust means: Devroye–Lerasle–Lugosi–Oliveira 2016 / Lugosi–Mendelson — with only a (1+α)-th moment the minimax deviation rate is n^{−α/(1+α)}; *no rate gain over the sample mean, only sub-Gaussian concentration*; Chaudhuri & Hill 2025 tail-trim the IPW summand and bias-correct via a tail index (limited overlap, not lift tests). Two-part models and their causal critique (Angrist 2001; Chen & Roth "Logs with zeros" 2024: separate the margins, Lee-bound the intensive one); Lee 2009 trimming bounds. Adaptivity: Stein 1956 / Bickel 1982 — the two-sample shift with unknown common density is adaptively estimable; HL ARE ≥ 0.864, 3/π at the normal. Decision layer: Feit & Berman's Test & Roll (sample size, not estimator choice); Azevedo et al. 2020 (fat tails in the *prior over innovations*, JPE); and the closest prior art, **Parikh et al. 2026 (arXiv 2607.23254)**: across Amazon RCT families, MSE is convex in the winsorization level and covariate adjustment + outcome-specific winsorization minimizes MSE, yet plain DIM minimizes *rollout regret* — "dramatic divergence between MSE and regret rankings" — reported empirically with no theory.

The subagent's gap list (confirmed unclaimed after search): no efficiency bound for the ATE under extensive-only or scale-shift restrictions and no statement that it is tail-free; no test-then-estimate procedure with a priced pretest; no regret-minimizing trimming rule with an identified bias envelope; no coupling of the Devroye rate with the margin decomposition; Trimmed Match's symmetric-residual trick not transported to user-level tests.

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — the margin decomposition of the bound and the tail-free restricted bounds (derived here; clean-room verified)

Write the ATE as τ = m_A[(p1−p0)κ + p0γ]. Three nested models:

- **M_U (unrestricted).** Hahn's bound with no covariates is V_U = [Var(Y(1)) + Var(Y(0))]/n ≈ 2p0 m_A²(1 + CV_V²)/n, attained by DIM. For lognormal baskets 1 + CV_V² = e^{s²}. With covariates the bound drops by E[σ²(X)]/σ²; the heavy part of σ² is the *idiosyncratic basket* Var(V | user), which no pre-period covariate predicts, so in the base DGP the covariate bound is only 4% below the no-covariate bound (E1: 0.664 vs 0.693) even though CUPED's R² is 0.12. **Lewis–Rao's floor is Hahn's bound and it is real.**
- **M_S (scale-family intensive margin, κ = 1).** F_I = F_A and always-buyers' baskets are scaled by (1+γ): the treated positives' basket law is F_A(·/(1+γ)). Then log V is a two-sample location-shift problem with unknown common density f; by Stein/Bickel the shift is adaptively estimable with information I_f (the Fisher location information of the log-basket density, 1/s² for lognormal), and the efficient influence function for τ is m_A[(1+γ)ψ_p1 + ψ_p0 + p1 ψ_γ] + (τ/m_A)ψ_m with ψ_γ the efficient score of the shift (moment-free) and ψ_m the pooled-mean score (weight τ/m_A ≈ 0.0025, negligible). Bound: V_S ≈ m_A²[2p0(1−p0)/n + p1²·(2/(n p0))/I_f] = (2p0 m_A²/n)[(1−p0) + 1/I_f] to first order in ℓ. Hodges–Lehmann attains (π/3)·(1/I_f) at the normal.
- **M_E (extensive-only, κ = 1, γ = 0).** τ = (p1−p0)m_A: V_E ≈ m_A²·2p0(1−p0)/n, plus the negligible (τ/m_A)²Var(m̂).

**The variance-ratio law (lognormal baskets, log-sd s).** Dividing by V_U:

  V_E/V_U → (1−p0)·e^{−s²},  V_S(HL)/V_U → (1 + (π/3)s²)·e^{−s²},  V_S(efficient)/V_U → (1 + s²)·e^{−s²}.

The law is p0-independent to first order and depends on the tail only through s. At s = 0.5 there is nothing to gain (0.78, 0.98); at s = 1.27 (CV_V = 2) the extensive-only estimator has one-fifth of DIM's variance and the scale model one-half; at s = 2 (CV_V ≈ 7) they have 1.8% and 9%. **E2** (n = 200k/arm, 60 reps, ±18% MC error on ratios): observed/predicted ext 0.777/0.779, 0.485/0.445, 0.221/0.199, 0.082/0.077, 0.018/0.018 at s = 0.5, 0.9, 1.27, 1.6, 2.0; scale-HL 1.04/0.98, 0.96/0.82, 0.63/0.54, 0.31/0.28, 0.093/0.095. A 300-rep own check at s = 1.27 gave 0.512 vs 0.536 and Var(γ̂) = 3.35e−4 vs the HL formula 3.38e−4; the clean-room (2000 reps) gave **0.187 ± 0.008 vs 0.189 and 0.531 ± 0.018 vs 0.536**, and at s = 2 numerators within 5% of theory but Var(DIM) under-measured by 13% even with 2000 reps (its own fourth moment is e^{6s²}) — the law is asymptotic and at s = 2 hard to *see*, which is itself a statement about how badly the unrestricted problem behaves. **E14** (p0 ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.2}): ext ratios 0.21/0.24/0.15 vs predicted 0.20/0.19/0.16 — p0-independent as derived. **E12**: HL attains the bound on a log-logistic basket law (ARE 1.15 ± 0.14; theory 1) and 0.83 ± 0.07 on lognormal (theory 3/π = 0.955, 1.8 MC se low); the median shift gives 0.80/0.58 (theory 0.75/0.64).

**What is tail-free and what is not.** Under M_S or M_E the *relative lift* L = ℓκ + γ involves no moment of F at all: ℓ from binomials, γ from ranks. The *absolute* ATE keeps one moment, m_A, but only as a common multiplicative scale (its sampling error enters with weight τ/m_A). The tail's infinite variance, when α < 2, therefore sits in a term with coefficient (p1−p0)/p0 ≈ ℓ relative to the leading term. **E3** (Pareto α = 1.5, 1.8, 2.5; n = 50k, 200k, 800k; 100 reps): the DIM error scale (1.4826·MAD across reps) shrinks with exponent −0.328 at α = 1.5 (theory −(1−1/α) = −0.333) and its ordinary sd is erratic (0.66 at 200k, 0.67 at 800k; kurtosis 5–28; normal-CI coverage 0.98–0.99 because the plug-in se is inflated by whichever outlier is in-sample), while ext and scale shrink at −0.46 and −0.50 with coverage 0.93–0.98; at n = 800k the se ratio is 12.6× (0.67 vs 0.053). Clean-room: −0.311 and −0.465 with 1500 reps, and the note that ext's (p1−p0)·(m̂ − m) stable component is ~10× below its Gaussian incidence term at 800k and would dominate only at astronomically large n. Robust means (median-of-means, Catoni, trimmed) cannot do this: Devroye et al.'s lower bound says the *unrestricted* rate is n^{−(1−1/α)} for any estimator; the √n rate here is bought by M_E's restriction, not by robustness.

### 3.2 Construction 2 — the winsorization path, the effect-location profile and the retention index (derived here)

Winsorizing at a common, pre-fixed cap c estimates τ_c := E[min(Y(1),c)] − E[min(Y(0),c)], an identified quantity that is not τ. Define the **effect-location profile** e(c) := τ_c/τ and the **retention index** R(c) := e(c)·√(v(∞)/v(c)), v(c) the per-user variance of min(Y, c). The path c ↓ 0 is the extensive-only estimator (min(Y,c) → c·1{Y>0}); c = ∞ is DIM.

Two exact lemmas about the decision layer (go iff τ̂ > 0, loss |τ| when wrong, τ̂ ~ N(·, σ²)):

- **Lemma A (additive bias, flat prior):** regret = (b² + σ²)/2. So for a fixed additive bias "minimize regret rather than MSE" is empty — regret *is* half the MSE. (**E4** quadrature: ratio 1.000 at six (b, σ) pairs; clean-room to 6 decimals.)
- **Lemma B (proportional bias, flat prior):** if τ̂ ~ N(e·τ, σ²), regret = σ²/(2e²) = v(∞)/(2R²): the retention index is the sufficient statistic, the MSE is improper (bias² ∝ τ² does not integrate), and attenuation is free whenever it buys more than proportional variance. (Quadrature exact; clean-room exact.) For a *threshold* decision L̂ > L0 > 0 the attenuated estimator must be de-biased by e(c) — under M_S, e(c) is a functional of F̂_A alone and hence tail-free — after which regret again depends only on R(c) (E4: identical regret for (e, σ) = (0.5, 0.5) and (1, 1)); left un-debiased, attenuation biases toward "no-go", which helps when the prior sits below the threshold (μ = 0: 0.058 vs 0.103) and hurts when above (μ = 2: 0.301 vs 0.103).

The profile decides the winsorization question (**E4**, population values from a 3M-user always-buyer sample; caps at pooled positive quantiles 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, 0.995, 0.999, 0.9999, ∞):

| effect model | e(c) | R(c) | best cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| extensive, κ = 1 | .28 .55 .69 .80 .93 .96 .99 1.0 1 | 2.31 1.98 1.77 1.60 1.32 1.24 1.13 1.06 1 | c → 0 (= EXT) |
| extensive, κ = 0.5 | .44 .71 .83 .90 .97 .99 1.0 1.0 1 | 3.56 2.57 2.12 1.81 1.39 1.28 1.14 1.06 1 | c → 0 |
| scale γ = 5% on always-buyers | .08 .29 .46 .60 .83 .89 .96 .99 1 | 0.68 1.06 1.17 **1.21** 1.18 1.15 1.10 1.05 1 | 95th pct (+21% se-equivalent) |
| tail: top decile +50% | 0 0 0 .15 .61 .73 .89 .97 1 | 0 0 0 0.30 0.87 0.95 1.02 1.03 1 | ≥ 99.9th pct; any lower cap *loses* |

So the industry's "cap at the 99th–99.9th percentile" heuristic is right only for scale-type effects, is far too timid for incidence-driven effects (where the gain is e^{s²/2} ≈ 2.6× in se at c → 0), and is harmful for tail-located effects. This also gives a derived reading of Parikh et al.'s divergence: MSE-optimal winsorization is aggressive when effects are small relative to noise (MSE ≈ v(c)), while regret is governed by R(c); the two rankings diverge exactly when e(c) falls faster than √v(c) — the signature of a tail-located effect profile, which is plausible for supply-chain outcomes. This is a prediction about their data, not a verified fact.

**E11** (null, 99th-percentile cap, 200 reps): a pooled arm-common cap keeps the two-sided FPR at 0.055 (lognormal and Pareto); per-arm caps give 0.095 and 0.12 — the Wang–Burke/Wicker pathology reproduced. The cap must be a fixed functional of the pooled data (or the pre-period).

### 3.3 Construction 3 (Round 3) — the rank-identified marginal-buyer basket ratio and the tail-free relative lift (derived here; the speculative step)

Round 2 (§4) shows M_E and M_S fail under *composition*: influenced buyers are not always-buyers. Under monotonicity and γ = 0 the treated positives are the mixture F1⁺ = w0 F_A + w1 F_I, so F_I = (F1⁺ − w0 F0⁺)/w1 is nonparametrically identified — but its *mean* is a heavy-tailed functional (it is DIM in disguise). Impose instead the **scale-mixture shape**: F_I(y) = F_A(y/κ). Then

  E[log V | treated positive] − E[log V | control positive] = w1·log κ  (exactly),

so log κ̂ = (mean-log difference)/ŵ1 is tail-free (needs only Var(log V) < ∞, true for lognormal and Pareto alike), and L̂ = ℓ̂·κ̂ is a tail-free relative lift whose only moment-free inputs are two binomials and two log-means. With an intensive effect the shift is w0 log(1+γ) + w1 log κ; computing κ̂ with γ = 0 gives κ̂ → κ(1+γ)^{w0/w1} — a 20× amplification at ℓ = 0.05 — but L̂ = ℓκ̂ → ℓκ + κγ + O((γ/ℓ)²), which equals the true L = ℓκ + γ to first order when κ ≈ 1: the rank shift measures the *total* intensive-margin lift and the split between κ and γ is irrelevant to L at first order (clean-room: κ̂ → 1.2202 at γ = 0.01, L̂ 0.0610 vs 0.0600; it also flagged that the expansion is in γ/ℓ, breaking down at γ = ℓ where L̂ = 0.133 vs 0.100). Precision: se(log κ̂) ≈ s_log√(1/n0⁺ + 1/n1⁺)/w1 ≈ 0.46 at n = 200k/arm and 0.19 at 1M; the estimator is exp-of-a-noisy-log, so it is median-unbiased for κ but mean-biased by ≈ exp(se²/2) − 1 (+11% at 200k), and the product ℓ̂·exp(shift·p̂1/Δp̂) is unstable when Δp̂ is noisy (CV 28% at 200k). A grid posterior over log κ with a weak prior N(0, 0.5²) and Δp integrated over its sampling distribution (`rank_kappa_post`) removes the instability at the price of prior shrinkage.

**E5/E6** (n = 200k/arm, 100 reps; MC se on bias ≈ sd/10): RANK-κ bias +0.06 ± 0.03 (+12%), +0.05 ± 0.02 (+17%), +0.10 ± 0.05 (+10%), +0.16 ± 0.07 (+11%) at κ = 0.5, 0.3, 1, 1.5 — the Jensen term — with sd 0.29, 0.21, 0.47, 0.69 against DIM's 0.76 (2.6×, 3.6×, 1.6×, 1.1× in se; the gain shrinks as κ grows because se(τ̂) ∝ κ·se(log κ̂)). At n = 1M: κ = 0.5 bias −0.012 ± 0.019, sd 0.121 vs DIM 0.354 (2.9×); κ = 1 sd 0.206 vs 0.355 (1.7×). Posterior version (E1, κ = 1): bias +0.006, sd 0.386 (1.86× vs DIM). Wald CI on L covers 0.87–0.93; a log-scale CI covers 0.94 (E7). Median κ̂ = 0.514, 0.317, 1.01, 1.46 at κ = 0.5, 0.3, 1, 1.5 (clean-room 0.498 ± 0.008 at κ = 0.5).

**Round 2 attacked** on four fronts. (a) *Composition* (E5): let influenced buyers spend κ = 0.5 of always-buyers. EXT's relative bias is (1−κ)/κ — +100% (+103% observed; bias/sd 1.8 at 200k, 4.8 at 1M), κ-ISO +95%, SCALE-HL −32% (its rank shift half-absorbs the composition), DIM unbiased; the exact finite-ℓ form is (1 − f_I(1−κ))/κ − 1 with f_I the influenced share of pooled positives (clean-room). At κ = 0.3 EXT is +237% and SCALE −133% (sign flip). The tail-free gain of M_E/M_S is therefore an *assumption purchase*, and the assumption is about people (marginal buyers' baskets), not tails. (b) *Is the restriction testable?* The Mann–Whitney test of F1⁺ = F0⁺ is tail-free but weak where it matters: power 0.40 at κ = 0.5 and 0.77 at κ = 0.3 with n = 200k/arm, 0.925 at κ = 0.5 with 1M (E5; null 0.02–0.05). At Lewis–Rao's small n the restriction cannot be checked from the experiment itself. (c) *Infinite variance* (E3): survived — §3.1. (d) *Tail-located effects* (E9; only the top decile of always-buyers spends +50%, τ = 5.04): DIM sees 105% of it (t 6.3), winsor-99 sees 72%, EXT 0.8%, SCALE 5%, RANK-κ is undefined (no incidence effect) — every restricted estimator is blind to an effect that lives in the tail, by construction. The tail-free detector is the *exceedance-count* test P(Y > c) by arm on a grid of c: z = 5.5 (power 1.0) at the 99th percentile, z = 0.4 at the 90th (a scale effect on the top decile does not move P(Y > c90)) — so a grid of exceedance tests, not a single one, is the pre-registerable guard.

**Round 3 refined** with Construction 3 — the rank-identified κ — which removes attack (a): consistent at every κ with 1.6–3.6× se gains at 200k (E5). **Round 3's attack** on it: (i) *shape*: if marginal buyers are the *small* buyers by selection (F_I = F_A truncated below its median, κ_eff = 0.17) rather than a scaled copy, the mean-log shift identifies the geometric-mean ratio, not the arithmetic one — κ̂ median 0.345 (2× too high), τ bias +122%, bias/sd 0.9, log-CI coverage 0.64 (E7; truncation at the 80th percentile: κ̂ 0.64 vs 0.38, coverage 0.72). A second-log-moment correction assuming F_I lognormal (κ_arith = κ_geo·exp((σ_I² − σ_A²)/2), both identified from the mixture) moves the median to 0.22 (truth 0.169) with IQR 0.16–0.37 — a partial, noisy repair. (ii) *γ*: an unmodelled 1% basket effect on always-buyers moves κ̂ to 1.22 (predicted exactly) and L̂ to 0.069 vs 0.060 at 200k (the population limit is 0.061; the rest is the product's finite-sample skew), τ bias +14% at bias/sd 0.3; at γ = 3% κ̂ → 1.74 and the sd explodes (5.9) — the posterior version is required there. (iii) *Heavier tail* (Pareto α = 1.5, κ = 0.7): RANK-κ sd 0.32 vs DIM 1.57 (4.8×), bias −0.03 ± 0.03, log-CI coverage 0.94; EXT biased +29% (lognormal +44%; law +42%).

**Round 4** produced no material revision of the constructions; it added the regret duel (E10) and the power control (E8), and settled the reading of the lemmas: the winsorization *level* should be chosen by R(c) under a pre-registered effect-location hypothesis, and the *estimator family* should be chosen by which margin one is willing to restrict. Round 5 (clean-room) confirmed all five claims and supplied three refinements now in the text (finite-ℓ bias form; the γ/ℓ expansion; the exp-of-log mean bias).

**Power / null control (E8;** n = 200k/arm, κ = 0.7, one-sided 5%, 200 reps, MC se 0.035): null false-positive rates DIM 0.05, EXT 0.025, incidence-z 0.025, winsor-99 0.05; power against a 5% incidence lift (3.5% revenue lift) DIM 0.255, winsor-99 0.355, EXT and incidence-z 0.955. The instrument discriminates.

**Clean-room** (independent implementation from claims only, no code shared): law at s = 1.27: 0.187 ± 0.008 (pred 0.189), 0.531 ± 0.018 (0.536), Var(d̂) 3.26e−4 ± 1e−5 (3.30e−4); at s = 2 numerators within 5%, denominator under-measured 13% by a 2000-rep MC; lemmas exact to 6 decimals; EXT composition bias +99.6 ± 1.7% at κ = 0.5, RANK-κ median 0.498 ± 0.008, DIM −1.4 ± 4.2%; γ-misattribution 1.228 (pred 1.220) at 2M/arm; Pareto slopes −0.311 (DIM) and −0.465 (EXT). One reconciled discrepancy: my E7 Pareto EXT bias (+29%) sits below the law's +42%; the clean-room's finite-ℓ correction and the downward finite-sample bias of Pareto sample means explain the direction; not material to any headline.

## 6. The decision layer: regret under a prior on the marginal buyer

**E10** puts the estimators into the go/no-go decision M6 asks about: L0 = 0.03 (breakeven relative lift), κ ~ U[0.3, 1.2] drawn per replication (so L = 0.05κ ∈ [0.015, 0.06]; 67% of prior mass above L0), loss |L − L0| when wrong, in units of baseline revenue per user.

| n/arm | DIM | EXT | RANK-κ | posterior | κ-ISO | always-go | never-go |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200k (600 reps) | 3.83 ± 0.28 | 3.24 ± 0.22 | **2.21 ± 0.22** | 2.54 ± 0.22 | 3.26 ± 0.23 | 2.62 | 9.81 |
| 1M (300 reps) | 2.25 ± 0.29 | 2.60 ± 0.25 | **0.75 ± 0.15** | 0.79 ± 0.15 | 2.46 ± 0.25 | 2.61 | 9.98 |

(regret × 10³). Three readings. First, at Lewis–Rao's 200k a DIM-based decision has *higher* regret than not running the experiment (3.83 vs 2.62): the estimate's noise flips more good campaigns off than it turns bad ones off. Second, MSE and regret rank differently exactly as Lemma A/B predict when bias is not additive-and-flat-prior: EXT's MSE is 2.5× better than DIM's (5.2 vs 12.9 ×10⁻⁴) yet its regret is no better (it says "go" almost always, because κ = 1 is assumed); the posterior has the best MSE at 200k (2.7) but RANK-κ the best regret. Third, at 1M the composition-robust rank estimator cuts regret 3× relative to DIM (0.75 vs 2.25; 5 MC se apart) while DIM itself is only 14% below always-go. The experiment's decision value in this regime comes almost entirely from the tail-free margin decomposition, not from sample size.

## 7. Devil's advocate against each headline claim

*"The unrestricted bound is Lewis–Rao's floor; covariates buy ≤ 4%."* The 4% is a property of my DGP, where the heavy tail is idiosyncratic basket size. Lewis–Rao saw up to 40% variance reduction from lagged sales in *some* experiments (frequent, persistent shoppers); with persistent basket sizes E[σ²(X)] can shrink much more, and nonparametric adjustment (Hahn's bound with a rich X) could beat CUPED's linear projection by more than the 3% seen here. The claim that holds generally is the structural one — the bound retains the within-user basket variance — not the 4%.

*"V_S/V_U → (1+s²)e^{−s²}."* It is a first-order law in ℓ and p0, for lognormal baskets, at π = ½. For other basket laws the factors become (1 + 1/I_f)/(1 + CV_V²) and the HL constant changes; for Pareto α < 2 the ratio is 0 in the limit but finite-sample behaviour is governed by pre-asymptotic mixing (E3's DIM slope −0.31, not −0.33). The clean-room could not distinguish the HL constant from the efficient one at s = 1.27 (3% apart), so "efficient" rests on Bickel's adaptivity theorem, not on simulation.

*"Relative lift is tail-free under M_S/M_E."* Only if the intensive effect is a *common scale* on always-buyers and the influenced stratum is a copy of them. Round 2 shows both can fail, and the honest gain from the restriction must be netted against the composition-bias law (1−κ)/κ, which at κ = 0.5 exceeds 100%. "Tail-free" is a statement about which *assumption* carries the tail, not that the tail has gone away: it has been moved into κ.

*"Regret = MSE/2; the retention index is sufficient."* Both lemmas assume a flat prior and normal errors. With an informative prior the threshold table (E4) shows un-debiased attenuation can be better or worse than de-biasing by a factor of 2–3 depending on where the prior sits relative to the breakeven. Under infinite variance the errors are not normal at any feasible n, and Lemma A does not apply to DIM at α = 1.5. The Parikh interpretation is a derived prediction, not a test of their data.

*"RANK-κ is composition-robust and cuts regret 3×."* Its consistency needs F_I to be a scale copy of F_A; Round 3's truncation attack produced a +122% bias with 64% coverage. Its γ-invariance is first-order in γ/ℓ at κ ≈ 1 only. The 3× regret gain is at n = 1M with a prior over κ that was uniform on [0.3, 1.2] — where EXT's assumption κ = 1 is wrong most of the time; under a prior concentrated at κ ≈ 1, EXT (or κ-ISO) would win and RANK-κ's extra variance would show. At 200k the gain over always-go is 16% (1.4 MC se): the honest statement is that RANK-κ makes a 200k-user experiment *barely* worth running under this prior, where DIM makes it worth *not* running.

*"Every restricted estimator is blind to tail-located effects."* True by construction, and DIM's 105% recovery of the tail effect at t = 6.3 shows the unrestricted estimator is the right tool when the effect is large and in the tail. The exceedance-grid guard has power 1.0 here because the effect was +50% on a decile; a +5% effect on the top percentile would be invisible to it and to DIM alike.

## 8. Limitations and failure modes

Single-period outcomes (no frequency margin; Lewis–Rao's decomposition has three margins — incidence, frequency, basket — and frequency is a count with its own semi-tail-free treatment). Person-level randomization without interference (auction/budget interference changes the estimand, dive 10). The strata model is monotone; defiers (users the ad *stops* from buying) break the mixture algebra. The pre-period covariate model is stylized. The rank estimator divides by ŵ1 ∝ Δp̂ and is unusable when the incidence effect is absent or negative — precisely the case where the intensive margin carries everything, so the two constructions are complementary, not nested. No real experiment library was touched: Berman–Feit's five catalog experiments and Lewis–Rao's 25 would let κ and s be read off directly, and a κ library across categories is the missing prior. The Trimmed-Match transport (trim a randomization-symmetric residual rather than the outcome at user level) was not attempted.

## 9. Next steps for a future session

1. **κ from an experiment library.** Fit the scale-mixture to Berman–Feit-style catalog data (or any lift library with per-user baskets): the distribution of κ across categories is the prior E10 needs and the empirical answer to "do marginal buyers spend less". Test the truncation alternative against the scale alternative with the second-log-moment statistic.
2. **The frequency margin.** Extend the decomposition to Y = Σ_k V_k over a Poisson/NB count: incidence and count are tail-free, only the basket is heavy; derive the three-margin bound and the corresponding rank estimator.
3. **Trimmed Match at user level.** Build a randomization-symmetric residual for user-level tests (e.g., pairwise differences within pre-period strata) so that symmetric trimming is unbiased under a homogeneous-lift assumption, and price the heterogeneity it tolerates (Chen & Au's Assumption 1 transported).
4. **Exceedance-grid guard as a pre-registered test.** Derive the joint null of the exceedance z's over a quantile grid (a Brownian-bridge functional) and its power against scale-on-top-decile alternatives, and wire it into dive 08's protocol as the trigger that switches the analysis from restricted to unrestricted.
5. **Regret-optimal cap under an informative prior.** Replace Lemma B's flat prior by dive 03/08's decision machinery: c\*(prior, e(·), v(·)) with e(c) estimated tail-free under M_S and a sensitivity band for composition; connect to Parikh et al.'s data if obtainable.
6. **Join to dive 14/15.** The κ law prices allocations in users × lift; the tail-free bound changes the user count by e^{s²} for incidence-driven effects — recompute dive 14's precision–lift frontier and dive 15's certification law under M_E.
7. **Bayesian strata model semiparametrically.** Replace Berman–Feit's lognormal components with the mixture-subtraction identification of F_I plus a smoothness prior; compare its posterior for κ to the rank estimator's.
