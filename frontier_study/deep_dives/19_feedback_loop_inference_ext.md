## 1. Problem statement

Dive 16 established that a certainty-equivalent (CE) allocator — spend set every refresh to the fitted profit optimum, no exogenous exploration — **stalls**: the design information freezes, the fitted curve wanders along a manifold through the true point at the current spend, and the model's own marginal ROAS at current spend echoes the breakeven target. It left three questions (its §8, items 2–5): can the stall be *detected* from logs, and what is the false-alarm rate of the "echo" (reported mROAS ≡ target for k refreshes) against a channel that is genuinely at breakeven? What is the stall's geometry with n channels under a budget, and what is the cheapest escape? Does the stall, the echo, and dive 16's policy-agnostic confidence sequence survive adstock, a demand confounder that also drives spend, and autocorrelated residuals?

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Single channel.** y_t = α + β H(a_t) + γ d_t + ε_t, H(a) = a^s/(a^s + K^s), a_t = x_t + λ a_{t−1} (adstock, λ = 0 by default), d_t an AR(1) demand state (ρ_d = 0.8) that is unobserved unless stated, ε_t independent N(0, σ²) or AR(1). Base parameters as in dive 16: β = 40, K = 5, s = 1, σ = 2; true optimum x\* = √(βK) − K = 9.14. Policy: 8-week burn-in at x\*(1 ± 0.25), then every R = 4 weeks a profile-OLS refit over a 40-point K grid (and a 9-point λ grid when λ ≠ 0) and spend set to the fitted long-run optimum x\*(θ̂) = (1−λ̂)(√(β̂K̂/(1−λ̂)) − K̂) (target mROAS 1), held until the next refit. Realised spend x_t = x\*(θ̂) + φ d_t + ξ_t with φ the *demand-chasing* coefficient (a marketer who raises spend into forecast demand) and ξ_t ~ N(0, v) exogenous exploration.
- **Diagnostics use only what a log contains:** the spend series, the outcome series, and — for one statistic — the set-point series x\*(θ̂_{t−1}) that an MMM-to-budget pipeline records.
- **Multichannel.** Local quadratic model y = α + Σ_j (b_j x_j − c_j x_j²/2) + ε with budget Σx = B (the second-order expansion of any separable Hill loop at its optimum; c = profit curvature); the CE allocator equalises fitted marginal returns b̂_j − ĉ_j x_j under the budget. A 3-channel Hill version (β = (30, 25, 20), K = (3, 5, 8), B = 15, nonlinear least squares, refit every 8 weeks) is the transport check. Exploration is budget-neutral: ξ ~ N(0, s²V) with V1 = 0. Feasible-direction projector P = I − 11′/n; H = diag(c); H_f = PHP with pseudo-inverse H_f⁺.
- **Loss.** Decision loss from a feasible-gradient error e is ½ e′H_f⁺e (the Newton-step loss of a quadratic objective); exploration's profit cost per week is ½ s² tr(HV).

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run; ~35 searches, full text of Ziemann–Sandberg 2021, Aoyama et al. 2026, Watson–Peters 2021, Heusch 2026). The stall itself is Keskin–Zeevi (2014, 2018): CE estimates converge with positive probability to an uninformative fixed point; their fix is a *policy* (a dispersion floor Σ(p_s − p̄)² ≥ κ√t; den Boer–Zwart's taboo interval), never a *test*. Lai–Wei (1982) give the consistency condition λ_min(Σx_tx_t′) → ∞ with log λ_max = o(λ_min), which is the natural basis for an information clock; Gustavsson–Ljung–Söderström (1977) show identifiability fails in closed loop under a fixed low-order regulator (the CE allocator) unless the regulator is complex/time-varying or an external excitation exists; Caines–Chan (1975) prove feedback-freeness ⇔ Granger non-causality, which makes "is spend policy-driven?" a Granger test of outcome → spend. Ziemann–Sandberg (2021/2024) formalise the multidimensional case: an *uninformative optimal policy* is one whose Fisher information is singular, and Fisher(π) = Fisher(π\*) + O(regret), so low regret forces near-singular information along a subspace whose dimension sets the regret lower bound. Inverse-Hessian exploration covariance with cost τn/2 independent of the Hessian's eigenvalues is claimed in robotics (Aoyama et al. 2026 §4.1, after Levine–Koltun 2013's Q_uu⁻¹ policy covariance); the *learning-side* corollary — that H⁻¹-shaped exploration makes precision isotropic in the profit metric so the cost of a target decision precision is curvature-free — was not found. Dynamic pricing with reference effects (Mgmt Sci 2022; EC'24) shows passive learning fails with probability one under carryover-like dynamics, the closest analogue to adstock. Heusch (2026a,b) supplies the endogenous-spend MMM benchmark (observational paid-search ROAS 10.6× vs true 4.2×, oracle controls still 8.4×) but its "performance chasing" chases realised outcomes, not the model's own fitted optimum. Anytime-valid inference under unmeasured confounding exists only as partial identification (Dalal et al. 2024); no AR-corrected e-process for regression with autocorrelated errors was found; no published statement of the marketing "echo". Judged unclaimed: an ex-post stall detector and its null; the echo false-alarm law; the learning-side curvature-whitening corollary and its switch-experiment representation; adstock-specific closed-loop stall; AR(1)-mixture confidence sequences for regression.

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — stall diagnostics from logs, and the echo false-alarm law

**(a) The exogenous clock (needs logged set-points).** Define D⊥_t = Σ_{u ≤ t} (x_u − x\*(θ̂_{u−1}))², the dispersion of realised spend around the policy's set-point. Under a pure CE loop D⊥ ≡ 0; under exploration of variance v it grows at exactly v per week (E11: per-week D⊥ = 0.0096/0.038/0.086/0.239/0.956 at v = 0.01/0.04/0.09/0.25/1.0 — the identity holds to Monte Carlo precision, and the raw spend variance overstates it by 3–30% because the set-point wanders). This is a bookkeeping identity, not a test — but it is the only clock that is guaranteed *unconfounded*: it counts dispersion that no belief and no demand state chose. Its threshold comes from dive 16's pricing law transported locally: a half-width w on mROAS by horizon T_h needs per-week exogenous dispersion v_min = (1.96σ/w)²/T_h (local linearisation; conservative by ≈ 2× in the Hill loop because burn-in and curvature information also count: predicted sd(m̂) 0.139 at v = 1, T = 208, measured 0.101). A stall alarm "D⊥/week < v_min" then has false-alarm rate P(χ²_W/W < v_min/v) over a window of W weeks and power 1 against the exact stall. E11 (W = 26, alarm at 0.05): alarm rate 1.00/1.00/0.83/0.02/0.00/0.00 at v = 0/0.01/0.04/0.09/0.25/1 — a sharp boundary between v = 0.04 and 0.09 as the χ²₂₆ law implies. Decision value of the same exploration: true-mROAS sd at the set-point 0.169 → 0.172 → 0.156 → 0.144 → 0.131 → 0.101, loss at the set-point 0.093 → 0.036 (E11), i.e. the alarm fires exactly where exploration has stopped buying anything.

**(b) The log-only pair.** Without set-points, an auditor has two established instruments. The *Lai–Wei clock*: λ_min of the design (1, H_K(x)) at T versus T/2. The *Caines–Chan feedback test*: Granger F of lagged outcomes on spend given lagged spend. E2 (T = 208, R = 300): growth ratio λ_min(T)/λ_min(T/2) = 1.01 (stall, 4-weekly refits), 1.003 (weekly), 1.47 (exploration), 1.40 (demand chasing), 1.72 (fixed spend + exploration); Granger rejection at 5%: 0.19 (stall), 0.54 (stall, weekly refits), 0.10/0.06 (exploration 4-weekly/weekly — the loop still feeds back through the set-point), 0.30/0.48 (chasing), 0.04 (fixed: the null holds). So the pair partitions the regimes only in principle: the clock separates {stall} from {exploration, chasing} cleanly, but the Granger test — the only log-only instrument that could separate exploration from chasing — has power 0.2–0.5 at MMM scales and, worse, is *not* a confounding test: demand chasing is feedback from a state, and once lagged spend (which carries the state) is conditioned on, lagged outcomes add little. The eigenvalue clock cannot tell learning from pseudo-learning (§3.2). **Negative result, stated plainly:** there is no log-only statistic in this dive that separates exogenous exploration from demand-driven spend; that separation needs either logged set-points (D⊥) or an observed demand proxy (E1's `chase_obs`).

**(c) The echo test and its false-alarm law.** The echo is an identity: at any refit the CE policy sets x so that β̂K̂/(x + K̂)² = 1, so the model's own mROAS *at the set-point* is 1.000 by construction, in every regime (E1, E10: sd ≤ 10⁻¹⁵ across R = 300 whenever the clip does not bind; 0.003 at s = 2 where the grid optimiser is used). This has two consequences. First, a dashboard that reports mROAS at *planned* spend cannot distinguish stall from learning — the echo is a property of the policy, not of the data. Second, the echo evaluated at *realised* spend does discriminate, and its false-alarm rate against a genuinely breakeven, non-stalled channel is a computable object. Let m̂_j be the reported mROAS at realised spend at refit j; under fixed spend x\* + ξ_j (ξ ~ N(0, v)) with 4-weekly refits, m̂_j − 1 = e_j + m̂′_j ξ_j where e_j is the slow model error at x\* (adjacent-refit correlation 0.99, the overlap correlation of dive 07) and m̂′_j ξ_j is the fitted slope times the *fresh* exploration draw. E3 (R = 400; clean-room R = 1,600): sd(m̂ − 1) = 0.21 at T = 208 with the two components 0.13 and 0.16 (true m′(x\*) = −0.141), adjacent-refit correlation only 0.36 (clean-room 0.39–0.44). The false-alarm rate FA_k(ε) = P(all k consecutive |m̂_j − 1| ≤ ε):

| ε | k = 1 | k = 3 | k = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.20 (clean-room 0.223 ± 0.010) | 0.05 (0.043 ± 0.005) | 0.02 (0.012 ± 0.003) |
| 0.02 | 0.09 (0.094) | 0.006–0.015 (0.0063 ± 0.002) | < 0.003 |
| 0.01 | 0.045 (0.048) | ≈ 0.001 (0.0006 ± 0.0006) | ≈ 0 |

and the stall hits every cell with probability 1. The obvious Gaussian theory — Z ~ N(0, Σ) with the empirical refresh sd and correlation — is **wrong by 5–150×** at k ≥ 3 (0.0085 vs 0.043 at ε = 0.05, k = 3; 8·10⁻⁵ vs 0.012 at k = 6; clean-room confirms the ratios): the product m̂′_j ξ_j is a scale mixture (β̂ and K̂ wander along dive 16's ridge, β̂ 5–95% [35, 224], so |m̂′| varies 3× across runs), and scale mixtures put far more mass near zero than a normal of the same variance. The refined law treats e as common across the k refits and ξ as iid: **FA_k(ε) = E_{e, m̂′}[(Φ((ε − e)/(|m̂′|√v)) − Φ((−ε − e)/(|m̂′|√v)))^k]**, with e ~ N(0, se_est²), se_est² = se² − E[m̂′²]v, and m̂′ drawn from the fitted slopes; it gives 0.056/0.026 (ε = 0.05, k = 3/6) and 0.006 (ε = 0.02, k = 3) against MC 0.043–0.05/0.012–0.018 and 0.006–0.015 — right order and within a factor 2, versus 5–150× for the Gaussian. Operational reading: "mROAS = 1.00 ± 0.02 for three consecutive quarterly refreshes" has a false-alarm rate below 1% against a truly breakeven channel with 10% spend dispersion, and a hit rate of 1 against the stall; with no dispersion at all the test is vacuous because the reported number is the policy's set-point returned as a measurement.

### 3.2 Construction 2 — confounded pseudo-learning (negative result; clean-room exact)

Demand chasing with an unobserved demand state (φ = 1.5, γ = 6 in the Hill loop, E1) is the dangerous regime, and it is the *opposite* of the stall on every clock: D_x grows 55× faster than the stall (7,650 vs 138 at T = 208), the eigenvalue clock reads "learning" (1.40), the fitted β̂ concentrates (5–95% [83, 134] vs [35, 238]) — and it concentrates on the wrong value: the loop raises spend 158% above the true optimum, the true mROAS at its spend is 0.27, and the model reports 1.01 ± 0.13. Making the demand state an observed covariate (E1 `chase_obs`) removes all of it (x bias +2%, mROAS bias 0.004). In the linear loop (E12; φ = 1, γ = 1, v = 0.3, T = 208) the naive interval's coverage is **0.000** (bias +0.66 on β = 2) and dive 16's policy-agnostic mixture confidence sequence has time-uniform coverage **0.007–0.017** (clean-room 0.016 at R = 1,500) — it is valid under *any* predictable spend policy and useless under a spend that carries an omitted regressor. Anytime validity is a statement about the sampling design, not about identification; the CS's guarantee is exactly as strong as the likelihood it mixes over. This is Heusch's endogeneity bias with a sharper diagnosis: the harm is not that the loop reacts to estimates (second-order, dive 16) but that it reacts to a state the model does not see; in Heusch's terms, "performance chasing" is harmless if it chases the model's own fitted optimum and catastrophic if it chases realised outcomes that contain demand.

### 3.3 Construction 3 — the multichannel stall geometry, the curvature-whitened exploration law, and its switch representation

**Geometry (E5, quadratic loop, n = 3, budget B = 10, R = 400).** The feasible-direction gradient error is frozen — rmse 1.469 at T/2 and 1.466 at T — while the allocator's own fitted marginal returns are equal across channels by construction (the multichannel echo: fitted spread 0 vs true spread 0.46) and the loss sits at 1.77 per week. Budget-neutral exploration (isotropic on the feasible subspace, s = 0.5) unfreezes it: 0.311 → 0.225, loss 0.174. In the 3-channel Hill loop (E8, refit every 8 weeks, T = 312) the same picture: true mROAS spread across channels at the set-point 0.127 (t = 156) → 0.120 (t = 312) with the fitted spread exactly 0 (equal-mROAS echo, sd 6·10⁻¹⁶), and t^{−1/4}-decaying budget-neutral exploration of unit amplitude buys only 0.116 → 0.111 and 13% of the loss (0.138 → 0.120) — enough to show the mechanism, not enough to call the escape cheap at this amplitude. **The budget direction is never learned.** With spend always on Σx = B, the regressors (1, x₁…x_n) are collinear and the total marginal value 1′g/n is identified by the quadratic form only: its error is 5.6 (median 3.4, against a true level of 2.3 — noise) at the stall, 1.06 under budget-neutral exploration, and 0.13 only when the exploration is *not* budget-neutral (E5 `explore_total`). A loop that explores within a fixed budget can learn how to split the budget and can never learn whether the budget is right; the "total mROAS" such a model reports is a functional-form artefact. This is dive 09's budget coupling seen from the identification side and Ziemann–Sandberg's information-singular subspace made concrete: under budget coupling the singular direction is 1.

**The passive law (derived; verified to its constant).** With spend x\* + ξ_t, ξ ~ N(0, s²V) on the feasible subspace, the local model gives Cov(ê) = σ²(T s²V)⁺ and E[L] = ½ tr(H_f⁺ Cov ê) = σ²/(2T s²) tr(H_f⁺V⁺). Fixing the per-week profit cost τ = ½ s² tr(HV) gives E[L] = σ² tr(HV) tr(H_f⁺V⁺)/(4Tτ), minimised over V by Cauchy–Schwarz at **V ∝ H_f⁺** with E[L]\* = σ²(n−1)²/(4Tτ) — independent of the curvature spectrum: **explore more in flat directions, and the cost of a target decision precision is curvature-free.** The penalties for the natural alternatives are exact ratios: isotropic V = P costs tr(H_f) tr(H_f⁺)/(n−1)², and V ∝ H_f (exploring stiff directions) costs Σλ² Σλ⁻²/(n−1)². E6 (c = (0.1, 0.4, 1.6), b = (3, 3, 3.5), τ = 0.2, T = 208, R = 300): mean loss 0.046/0.026/0.134 for iso/H⁺/H, ratios **1.77 vs theory 1.75** and **5.2 vs theory 6.25** (the shortfall is the burn-in's information, common to all designs; clean-room 1.76 and 5.39, derivation confirmed). The cheapest escape from the multichannel stall is therefore not "a switch experiment" but a *shape*: dispersion whitened by the profit Hessian.

**Switch representation (theorem for separable channels).** Dive 04's budget-neutral switch experiment between channels i and j is the rank-1 design d_ij d_ij′, d_ij = e_i − e_j. For diagonal H = diag(c) the constrained inverse is M = C⁻¹ − C⁻¹11′C⁻¹/S, S = Σ_k 1/c_k, whose off-diagonals are −1/(c_i c_j S) < 0, so **H_f⁺ = Σ_{i<j} w_ij d_ij d_ij′ with w_ij = 1/(c_i c_j S) > 0** exactly (E7: residual 10⁻¹⁵ for 200 random lognormal curvature vectors at each n = 3…6; c = (0.1, 0.4, 1.6) gives w = 1.905, 0.476, 0.119; clean-room exact to 10⁻¹⁴). The optimal exploration is a rotating schedule of pairwise switches whose squared amplitudes scale as 1/(c_i c_j): switch most between the two flattest channels. The representation is *not* generic: with cross-channel interactions (non-diagonal H) the NNLS residual is nonzero in 4.5%/28%/65% of random Hessians at n = 3/4/5, and the stiff-direction design H_f (the wrong one) is representable in only 74%/39%/24% — the pairwise-switch dictionary has exactly n(n−1)/2 elements, the dimension of the symmetric forms on the feasible subspace, so representability is a sign condition, not a rank condition.

**Inside the loop the law bends (partial failure, reported as found).** Adding the same three designs at the same τ to the CE loop (E6, R = 600): by *median* loss the ranking survives with attenuated ratios (iso/H⁺ 1.07, H/H⁺ 1.75, vs 1.63/3.78 passive medians); by *mean* it reverses (0.76, 0.91) because the loop's loss distribution is heavy-tailed (mean/median 4–6; q99 = 2.1–2.6 against a median of 0.05; not driven by boundary hits — excluding them leaves the mean unchanged). Two attacks on the interpretation: (i) the loop's own wobble is already H⁻¹-shaped (whitened wobble eigenvalues (0.38, 1.28) without exploration), so a water-filling complement V_fill = H^{−1/2}(w_max I − W_loop)_+H^{−1/2} should beat H⁺ — it does not (median 0.068 vs 0.045; mean 0.23 vs 0.25, inside 1 se): **refuted**; (ii) a passive design given an *exogenous* wobble of the loop's measured covariance reaches loss 0.012 against the loop's 0.19–0.25 (mean) / 0.045 (median) — the same dispersion is worth 4–20× less when the loop generates it, because the loop samples where its own error is (x_dev = H⁺e) and at CE gain the O(g²) terms dive 16 called second-order are not small (g·sd/√v ≈ 7 here). Dive 16's "dispersion informs" is therefore a weak-feedback statement; at CE gain, dispersion that the estimate chose is mostly not information.

### 3.4 Construction 4 — adstock does not rescue the stall and iid exploration does not identify it (E4)

With true λ = 0.5 and a 9-point λ grid profiled at each refit (T = 312), the stall is unchanged: own long-run mROAS 1.000 (sd 10⁻¹⁶), true mROAS sd at the set-point 0.248 (t = 156) → 0.240 (t = 312), D_x 132 → 142, loss 0.124 — and the fitted λ̂ is frozen at burn-in precision (sd 0.092 at both times, mean 0.478). Decaying exploration ξ ~ N(0, (1.5·t^{−1/4})²) cuts the mROAS error (0.240 → 0.184) and the loss (0.124 → 0.076) but leaves λ̂'s sd at 0.092: **iid weekly dispersion does not identify carryover**, exactly as dive 01 found for the passive design (the adstock transient needs block pulses of length ≈ 3/(1−λ); removing it collapses λ_min ~700×). The echo holds for the long-run mROAS at steady state because the CE set-point is defined through the fitted long-run multiplier; the loop learns the value of β̂ĤH(a_∞) along a (β, K, λ) ridge and reports the target back. Robustness (E10, T = 208): the stall/echo numbers are unchanged at weekly and quarterly refits (growth 1.003/1.048), σ = 4 (true-mROAS sd 0.248), s = 2 (echo 1.000 ± 0.003), and across five seeds (E13: true-mROAS sd 0.155–0.170, D_x 129–152).

### 3.5 Construction 5 — the AR(1)-mixture confidence sequence (assembly; established by simulation)

Dive 16's CS mixes a normal-inverse-gamma prior over (θ, σ²) and projects by sup over σ². Autocorrelated residuals were its weakest cell (0.978 at AR(1) = 0.6). The repair is exact in the martingale argument: the conditional density p(y_t | y_{t−1}, x_{1:t}, θ, σ², φ) = N(α(1−φ) + β(x_t − φx_{t−1}) + φ y_{t−1}, σ²) is a valid one-step likelihood for every predictable x, so a mixture over (θ, σ², φ) with φ on a uniform grid is a nonnegative martingale and Ville applies; projecting by sup over (σ², φ) gives **CS_t = {θ : min_φ RSS_φ,t(θ) ≤ R\*_t}** with R\* computed from the φ-mixed NIG evidence. E9 (smooth linear loop g = 10, v = 1, R = 300; 12-point φ grid on [−0.2, 0.9]): time-uniform coverage 1.00 at AR = 0/0.6/0.8 against 1.00/0.99/**0.927** for the iid mixture (naive fixed-time 0.947/0.80/0.66), and it is *narrower* where it matters — median half-width 0.355 vs 0.445 at AR = 0.6 and **0.204 vs 0.352** at AR = 0.8 (the GLS efficiency of prewhitening), at a 6% width price at AR = 0. BL58's second half is closed in the linear loop.

## 4. Attack-and-refine history

**Round 1 (construct).** Built the diagnostic trio (D⊥ clock, Lai–Wei clock, Granger test), the echo test with a Gaussian false-alarm theory, the quadratic multichannel loop, and the curvature-whitened exploration law with its prediction V ∝ H⁺ inside the loop. First numbers: the echo hit rate on stalled runs came out **0** — a bug (the "stalled" run had exploration switched on) — and Granger power in the stall was 0.19.

**Round 2 (attack).** (a) The Gaussian FA theory was refuted by its own Monte Carlo (5–150× too small at k ≥ 3); diagnosis: the refresh error is a scale mixture through the wandering fitted slope, and adjacent-refit correlation is 0.36, not the overlap 0.99, because the exploration term is fresh each refit. (b) Granger's weak power was traced partly to grid discreteness in the optimiser (set-points rounded to 0.05 hid the feedback) — replaced by the closed-form optimum — and mostly to the feedback being small relative to exploration; at weekly refits power is 0.54. The test's inability to flag demand chasing (0.30) was recognised as structural, not a power problem. (c) The in-loop exploration law came out **reversed** (H⁺ worst). Attack on the experiment: the exploration budget was tiny relative to the burn-in pulses, and channel 3's true optimum was negative (clipped) — fixed (b = (3, 3, 3.5), τ = 0.2), after which the passive law verified to its constant and the loop reversal persisted. (d) The metrics for learning (E4, E8, E11) were contaminated by evaluating the true mROAS at the *explored* spend rather than the set-point, which made exploration look harmful; separated into the echo at realised spend (the auditor's object) and the error at the set-point (the decision object).

**Round 3 (refine).** (a) Scale-mixture FA law derived and tested (within 2× of MC; clean-room confirms the Gaussian failure and the MC values at R = 1,600, and flags that two small cells at R = 400 were overstated 2–5× — the table reports the clean-room numbers). (b) The loop reversal was dissected: heavy tails (mean/median 4–6) — medians restore the ranking with attenuated ratios; the water-filling complement was built as the natural refinement and **refuted**; the exogenous-wobble control located the loss in the feedback (4–20×), not in the dispersion shape. (c) The switch representation was derived in closed form after NNLS returned exact fits for every diagonal case, and its generic failure for non-diagonal Hessians was mapped. (d) The budget-direction non-identification was added as the multichannel analogue of the stall and verified with a non-budget-neutral control.

**Round 4 (attack again).** Seed sweep (E13), refresh/σ/s sweep (E10), adstock (E4), AR-CS at AR = 0.8 (E9): no material revision. The 3-channel Hill run at T = 312 (E8) narrowed the "frozen" claim to "slowly shrinking" (0.127 → 0.120) — stated as such. Two consecutive rounds without material revision; stopped.

## 6. Limitations and failure modes; devil's advocate

*Against the diagnostics:* the D⊥ clock is only as good as the set-point log — a pipeline that overwrites set-points with realised spend (common) destroys it, and implementation noise counted as "exogenous" is genuinely informative only if it is independent of demand, which finance-driven cuts are not. The echo test at realised spend needs dispersion to have any power, and its FA law is a two-component approximation with a common slow error; it is within 2×, not exact. The Granger test is a feedback test with power 0.2–0.5 here and is silent on confounding by construction; a reader who hoped for a log-only confounding detector gets a proof that this dive did not find one. *Against the confounding result:* φ = 1.5 with γ = 6 is a strong chaser; at φ = 0.5, γ = 2 the mROAS bias is still +0.46 (E14) — the regime is not knife-edge, but the numbers are illustrative, not calibrated to any advertiser. *Against the exploration law:* it is a local quadratic law with a scalar noise; in the loop it holds by median only, the heavy tail is real and unexplained beyond "the loop samples where its error is", and the 4–20× productivity gap between exogenous and loop-generated dispersion is measured at one gain. The switch theorem needs a separable (diagonal) profit Hessian; cross-channel synergies break it in a third to two-thirds of random cases at n = 4–5. *Against the budget-direction claim:* it is a statement about first-order identification; the quadratic form does identify 1′g through curvature, just 8× worse than a total pulse, and a model with a fixed functional form will report a confident number there. *Against the adstock result:* one λ, a coarse grid, iid exploration only — the dive shows what does *not* identify λ and defers to dive 01 for what does. *Against the AR-CS:* established by simulation at three AR values in the linear loop; the φ grid is a design choice, the width comparison uses the profile φ, and heteroskedastic or MA errors are untested. *Against novelty:* every ingredient is known (Lai–Wei, Caines–Chan, Keskin–Zeevi, Levine–Koltun's Q_uu⁻¹, Robbins mixtures); the contributions are the FA law and its Gaussian failure, the learning-side whitening corollary with its exact switch representation and its in-loop breakdown, the budget-direction non-identification, the anytime-validity-≠-identification demonstration, and the AR(1) projection.

## 8. Next steps for a future session

1. **A log-only confounding detector.** The gap this dive leaves open: with set-points unlogged, nothing here separates exploration from demand chasing. Candidates: a Hausman contrast between the loop-window slope and a burn-in/pulse-window slope (dive 11's leak test transported), or the sign of the O(g) skew coefficient of dive 16 as a feedback fingerprint.
2. **The in-loop exploration law with feedback.** Derive the O(g²) correction to Cov(ê) when x_dev = H⁺e, explain the heavy tail (a Cauchy-like ratio at the CE fixed point?), and find the design that is optimal *given* the loop's error-correlated wobble — the water-filling guess failed; the answer may be to explore in the whitened direction where the loop's wobble is *most* error-correlated, not least dispersed.
3. **Switch schedules under synergy.** For non-diagonal Hessians the pairwise dictionary is insufficient in a sign sense; add triple switches (d_ij + d_jk directions) and characterise the cone.
4. **The budget-direction escape.** Price a periodic total-budget pulse (non-neutral) in dive 03's EVSI against the loss from never learning 1′g; connect to dive 09's budget-coupled deadband, whose Γ matrix presumes 1′g is known.
5. **Echo FA law exactness.** Replace the two-component approximation by the exact distribution of (β̂, K̂) along the ridge (dive 01's δ⁴ ridge geometry) to make FA_k(ε) a computed rather than simulated number, and wire the k-refresh echo into dive 08's protocol as an "information clock stopped" trigger with derived α.
6. **AR-CS in the Hill loop and under MA/heteroskedastic errors**; test whether the prewhitened radius can be tuned (BL55) jointly.
7. **Empirical:** run the D⊥ clock, eigen-clock and echo test on Heusch's endogenous-spend benchmark, where set-points are known by construction.
