## 1. Problem statement

Multi-touch attribution (Shapley/"data-driven" attribution, Markov removal effects, last-touch) allocates conversions to channels from the observational journey data alone. M8 asks two things: (i) characterize the class of journey-value functions for which Shapley attribution equals an average of per-touch causal effects; (ii) prove that no observational attribution rule can jointly satisfy efficiency, symmetry and a counterfactual-consistency axiom under confounded exposure.

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Units and exposure.** Unit i has an exposure set Z_i ∈ {0,1}^n (which of n channels touched it) and potential outcomes {Y_i(z) : z ∈ {0,1}^n}. The *observational law* is P = law of (Z, Y(Z)), summarized by P(S) := P(Z = 1_S) and v_obs(S) := E[Y | Z = 1_S] for each set S. (Journeys are treated as sets, not ordered paths; see §7.)
- **The cross-journey table.** c(S, A) := E[Y(1_A) | Z = 1_S] for A ⊆ S: what the users who received journey S *would* have converted at if only the sub-journey A had reached them. Its diagonal c(S, S) = v_obs(S) is observed; everything off the diagonal is not.
- **Three games.** v_star(A) := E[Y(1_A)] (everyone exposed to exactly A — the game "data-driven attribution" implicitly targets); v_pop(A) := E[Y(Z ∧ 1_A)] = Σ_S P(S) c(S, S∩A) (channels in A keep their realized delivery, the rest are switched off); and the confounding-bias game b := v_obs − v_star.
- **Rules.** Population-game Shapley Sh(v_obs) (Shao–Li 2011 style); journey-level DDA_j := Σ_S P(S) Sh_j(v_obs|_S) (Shapley within each realized journey, credits summed — the Google DDA / Singal et al. construction); removal effect τ_j := E[Y(Z) − Y(Z − e_j)] (what a single-channel ghost-ad holdout measures; the Markov "removal effect" is its observational proxy); equal split of each conversion among its touches.
- **Axioms.** (E) causal efficiency: Σ_j φ_j = E[Y(Z) − Y(0)] (credits sum to the realized incremental lift); (N) causal null: if Y_i(z) does not depend on z_j for every unit, φ_j = 0; (S) symmetry (label equivariance); (L) linearity in the potential-outcome structure. An *observational rule* is any map φ(P) ↦ ℝⁿ.
- **Benchmark DGP** (for numbers): U ~ N(0,1); Z_j | U ~ Bern(σ(a_j + g_j U)) with a = (−0.5, −1.0, −1.5), g = (0.4, 1.0, 1.6) (channel 3 is the "retargeting" channel: strongest targeting, lowest reach); Y(z) | U ~ Bern(σ(−2.5 + U + 0.5 z₁ + 0.5 z₂ + 0·z₃)) — channel 3 is causally null. All population quantities are computed exactly by Gauss–Hermite quadrature.

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run) plus the catalogs: Shao–Li 2011, Zhao et al. 2018, Anderl et al. 2016 and Google's DDA all define the value function observationally (v_obs) and never state an identification condition. Singal, Besbes et al. (Mgmt Sci 2022) give the only axiomatic treatment with a counterfactual flavor — a "counterfactual null player" and "counterfactual efficiency" axioms uniquely characterizing CASV = SV(M) − SV(M_cf) — but the counterfactual is *model-internal* (a fitted Markov chain), so their axioms are satisfiable precisely because exposure is not confounded in the model. Berman 2018 shows equilibrium divergence between attributed and causal contribution as an incentive result (publishers exploit the baseline), with Shapley on an *interventional* revenue function as the benchmark. The explanation literature (Sundararajan–Najmi's "conditional Shapley violates dummy"; Janzing et al.; Heskes et al.; Frye et al. drop symmetry to inject causal order; Bilodeau et al. PNAS 2024: complete + linear attributions cannot test counterfactual model behaviour) is structurally analogous but concerns a fixed function, not a confounded treatment. Egami–Imai identify causal interactions (AMIE) from full factorials; a JASA 2025 paper recovers Shapley from fractional factorials under interaction sparsity — computational, not causal. Empirically, observational methods overstate RCT lift by ~3× in half of Gordon et al.'s 15 studies (Study 9: 6.6% exposure rate → 1,306% vs 2.4%), and DML/SPSM in "Close Enough?" give 83%/173% vs 29% median lifts. The subagent found none of the four target claims below in the literature: (a) the observational = interventional + bias-game decomposition, (b) the impossibility theorem, (c) the holdout-design rank condition for Shapley credit, (d) the targeting-intensity bias law.

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — the decomposition identities and the journey-level lemma (established here; clean-room exact)

Shapley is linear, so for the population-game rule

  **Sh(v_obs) = Sh(v_star) + Sh(b), b(S) = E[Y(1_S) | Z = 1_S] − E[Y(1_S)].**

Sh(v_star) is by construction a Shapley-weighted average of per-touch causal effects (v_star(B∪j) − v_star(B) is the effect of adding j to context B). So **the class asked for in M8(i) is exactly {v : Sh(v − v_star) = 0} = v_star + ker(Sh)**, an affine subspace of codimension n in the 2ⁿ-dimensional game space. In Harsanyi-dividend coordinates the kernel is {b : Σ_{T∋j} d_b(T)/|T| = 0 ∀j}; e.g. symmetric bias games b(S) = h(|S|) with h(n) = h(0), or any bias that is a pure constant (uniform selection). Targeting-generated confounding lands in the kernel only by measure-zero cancellation (§3.4).

For the journey-level rule the relevant target is not v_star but v_pop, and the bias is a *family* of games indexed by the realized journey:

  **DDA_j − Sh_j(v_pop) = Σ_S P(S) Sh_j(b_S), b_S(A) := c(A, A) − c(S, A) for A ⊆ S.**

Derivation: v_pop(B) = Σ_S P(S) c(S, S∩B); the game B ↦ c(S, S∩B) is the subgame on S with the players outside S null, and null players do not change the others' Shapley values, so Sh_j(v_pop) = Σ_S P(S) Sh_j(A ↦ c(S, A)) on players S; DDA replaces c(S, A) by c(A, A). Because b_S(S) = 0, efficiency of the bias family gives Σ_j Sh_j(b_S) = −b_S(∅) = c(S, ∅) − c(∅, ∅) — the classic baseline selection bias — so **DDA's total credit exceeds the realized lift by exactly E[Y(0)] − E[Y(0) | Z = ∅]**.

**Journey-level lemma.** If exposure is unconfounded (c(S, A) = c(A, A) for all A ⊆ S), then DDA = Sh(v_pop) *exactly*: journey-level Shapley on observational conversion rates is the efficient, symmetric, causal-null allocation of the realized lift. (E1: max|DDA − Sh(v_pop)| = 2.8e−17 at g = 0.) The population-game rule is *not*: Sh(v_star) allocates the lift of a counterfactual world in which every user sees every channel, so it ignores reach — with equal per-exposure effects and reach (0.38, 0.18, 0.08) its shares are (⅓, ⅓, ⅓) while the causal shares are (0.586, 0.291, 0.123) (E22). Shao–Li-style attribution mis-allocates even without confounding.

Benchmark numbers (E1; clean-room reproduced to all digits): Sh(v_pop) = (0.0230, 0.0220, 0.0000), Σ = 0.04497 = realized lift; τ = (0.0241, 0.0230, 0), Σ = 0.0471 (removal effects double-count the small positive interaction); DDA = (0.0274, 0.0365, 0.0271), Σ = 0.0911 = E[Y] − v_obs(∅); Sh(v_obs) = (0.104, 0.154, 0.133). The null channel gets 30% of DDA credit and ranks second on population Shapley; the ranking of the two real channels is inverted by both observational rules.

### 3.2 Construction 2 — the impossibility theorem (established here; two proofs, one reproduced)

**Theorem.** Let n ≥ 2 and let 𝒟 be any class of DGPs closed under the swap construction below. No observational rule φ(P) satisfies (E) and (N) on 𝒟. Symmetry and linearity are not needed; the conclusion survives (i) restricting 𝒟 to monotone targeting (g > 0), monotone confounding (β > 0) and non-negative effects, (ii) adding a global holdout (the realized total lift is then known), and (iii) restricting to a smooth single-index parametric family.

*Proof (nonparametric, principal-strata form).* Take the latent type to be the exposure set itself (deterministic delivery given type) with type-specific potential outcomes: any table {c(S, A)} with the observed diagonal is realized by some DGP with the same observational law P. Given P, construct for each j a table with j causally inert (c(S, A) = c(S, A∖j) for all A ⊆ S) by choosing the off-diagonal freely, and choose the off-diagonals so all n tables have the same total lift Δ = Σ_S P(S)[c(S,S) − c(S,∅)] > 0. Every such DGP has observational law P, so φ(P) is one vector; (N) forces φ_j(P) = 0 for every j; (E) forces Σ_j φ_j(P) = Δ > 0. Contradiction. ∎

*Proof 2 (within a smooth family — the one that matters for practice).* E15: with n = 2, Gaussian U, logistic exposure and outcome links, DGP A (a = (−0.5, −1.5), g = (0.6, 1.8), β₀ = −2, β = 1.2, θ = (0.8, 0): channel 2 null) and DGP B (a = (−0.947, −1.036), g = (2.779, 0.517), β₀ = −1.724, β = 1.063, β₂ = −0.193, θ = (0, 1.001): channel 1 null) have **identical** P(Z = 1_S, Y = y) on all 8 cells (max diff 5.6e−17; clean-room 1.4e−17 with B's parameters reproduced to 4 decimals) and identical realized lift 0.0502. Under A, Sh(v_pop) = (0.0502, 0); under B, (0, 0.0502). Both have g > 0, β > 0, θ ≥ 0. Every observational rule — DDA (0.0664, 0.0523), population Shapley (0.200, 0.213), Markov, last-touch, equal split — returns the same answer for both. A global holdout measures 0.0502 in both and cannot help.

What the theorem *is*: a statement that the identified part of the cross-journey table is its diagonal, and every causal allocation axiom pulls on the off-diagonal. What it is *not*: a statement that the Shapley operator is at fault — Singal et al.'s CASV axioms are all satisfiable *given* the off-diagonal; the failure is identification, not axiomatics.

### 3.3 Construction 3 — sensitivity bounds and the attribution robustness value (derived here; new)

Because Sh_j(v_pop) = Σ_{S, A⊆S} coef_j(S, A)·c(S, A) is linear in the table with signed coefficients (Shapley weights × P(S)), any cell-wise sensitivity model on the unidentified cells gives *sharp* interval bounds by plugging the upper cell bound where the coefficient is positive and the lower bound where it is negative. Three models, parametrized like Rosenbaum/Tan odds ratios on the cross-journey selection:

- **Global box Γ:** odds c(S,A) ∈ odds c(A,A)·[1/Γ, Γ] for every A ⊊ S.
- **Channelwise Γ_k:** odds c(S,A) ∈ odds c(A,A)·Π_{k∈S∖A}[1/Γ_k, Γ_k] — the log-odds selection shift is additive in the *missing* channels, which is exactly what the single-index first-order model implies (§3.4); the analyst sets one number per channel ("how much more intent does being targeted by retargeting reveal?").
- **Monotone (positive selection):** c(S,A) ≥ c(A,A) — users who received more touches have at least the baseline of users who received fewer; keeps only the upper factor.

The smallest channelwise parameters consistent with a DGP are an LP (minimize Σ log Γ_k s.t. every cell's log-odds ratio ≤ Σ_{k∈S∖A} log Γ_k); for the benchmark Γ_k* = (1.28, 1.82, 2.54) while the global Γ* = 5.31 (one extreme cell dominates). At the true parameters (E5, E16): global box widths (0.16, 0.15, 0.13) — 7× the credit; channelwise (0.054, 0.063, 0.066); channelwise + monotone (0.035, 0.042, 0.045) — 4.6× tighter than the box. The truth sits at 0.22–0.44 of the way up the channelwise intervals across four targeting regimes (0.37–0.60 for the box), i.e. the cell-wise bounds are conservative by a further factor ≈2–3 relative to the single-index truth.

**Attribution robustness values** (the Cinelli–Hazlett analogue, computed from the point-identified DDA allocation): the Γ at which channel j's interval first contains zero, and the Γ at which the top-2 DDA ordering becomes ambiguous. Benchmark: RV₀ = (1.6, 1.85, 1.65) channelwise, (1.8, 2.3, 2.0) box; **the ranking robustness is only Γ = 1.10 (channelwise) / 1.12 (box) / 1.20–1.26 with monotone selection** — a 10–25% cross-journey selection odds ratio is enough to make DDA's channel ranking uninterpretable, while the true Γ_k* run 1.3–2.5.

### 3.4 Construction 4 — the targeting-intensity bias law (derived here; operator form verified, closed form refuted then repaired)

Gaussian U with log-linear tilt: P(Z = 1_S | U)/P(S) ≈ 1 + U·L(S), L(S) = Σ_k g_k(s_k − ē_k), ē_k = σ(a_k). Hence to first order in the targeting slopes

  b(S) ≈ β_S·L(S), β_S := E[U·p(1_S, U)] (the outcome's U-slope under exposure S);  b_S(A) ≈ β_A[L(A) − L(S)] = −β_A Σ_{k∈S∖A} g_k.

Both are *operator* laws: Sh(b) ≈ Sh(β_·L) and DDA bias ≈ Σ_S P(S) Sh(b_S). E3: the operator predictions are within 1–4% of the exact bias for targeting scale ≤ 0.2 (g ≤ 0.32) and within 10% up to g ≈ 0.6, then over-predict by up to 2× at the benchmark scale (the logistic tilt saturates). If the outcome slope is exposure-invariant (β_S ≡ β̄) the games are additive and the law collapses to the memorable closed forms **Sh_j(b) ≈ β̄·g_j** and **DDA_j − Sh_j(v_pop) ≈ β̄·g_j·P(j ∈ Z)**: a channel's credit inflation is its own targeting slope times the confounder's outcome slope (times its reach for journey-level DDA), independent of its true effect and of the other channels. The null channel's DDA credit grows monotonically in g₃ (E2: 0 → 0.0057 → 0.0126 → 0.0201 → 0.0271 → 0.0394 for g₃ = 0 … 2.5, reaching 41% of total credit), and the population-game rank of a null channel rises from 3rd to 1st at g₃ ≈ 2.

## 4. Attack-and-refine history

**Round 1** built §3.1–3.4 with the closed-form law "Sh_j(b) ≈ β g_j" and box-Γ bounds, plus a discrete-U swap.

**Round 2 (attack).**
- *The closed-form law is wrong even as g → 0.* E3 (first version): ratios exact/closed-form converge to 1.37, 1.08, 1.01 for the three channels, not 1. Cause: the outcome U-slope β_S varies 1.7× across exposure sets in a logistic model (more touches → higher rate → steeper slope), so b is a *product* of a level-dependent slope and an additive term, not additive. Cross-channel leakage: channel 1's bias carries other channels' targeting through β_S. E11 confirms the failure pattern is generic (probit, bimodal U, skewed U, U-dependent persuasion: closed-form ratios 0.15–1.76 even at scale 0.1) while the *operator* law holds to ±5% everywhere at scale 0.1 and ±10% at 0.25, except U-dependent persuasion at scale 1 where DDA-bias/1st = −2.0 for the weak channel (sign of the first-order bias wrong: higher-order terms dominate a small bias).
- *The pairwise-cap design must be biased when 3-way interactions exist.* E18: with 3 active channels and no explicit synergy the 3-way dividend is −0.0003 and the design bias is 0.0000 (sd 0.0005 at N = 2M); with strong pairwise synergy (θ₁₂ = θ₂₃ = 2) the 3-way dividend is −0.019 and the bias is 0.0016 on every channel — uniform (the closed form's error is d₁₂₃/6 per channel, E17: −0.00005 in the benchmark), so rankings survive; it is 3× the sd only under synergy strong enough to dominate the main effects.
- *Is the impossibility "too easy" — doesn't a parametric single-index family with n ≥ 3 over-identify by counting?* Yes, and this is the honest boundary. E19: at n = 3 the rigid logit/logit/Gaussian family (11 parameters, 15 observable degrees of freedom) admits no exact swap: the best ch1-null fit to the ch3-null truth leaves a max cell mismatch of 7.0e−3 (≈2% of the cell), with or without a 2-component mixture for U. So functional form identifies the allocation at n ≥ 3 the way Heckman selection identifies without an exclusion restriction — detectably only with ~10⁵+ users per journey cell and only if the family is right; with nonparametric U (principal strata) the swap always exists. The theorem is nonparametric; parametric identification is by functional form alone.
- *Exchangeable targeting should be rank-preserving.* Refuted in the exact sense: with equal a and g the bias spread is 10% of its level (E13, from the same β_S variation), though rankings survived. A 50% gap in targeting slope (g₂ = 1.5 vs g₁ = 1.0) overturns a 50% gap in true effect (0.6 vs 0.4) (E13).
- *Sparse journey cells.* E21: at n = 6 (64 journeys) with 44% of cells under 30 users at N = 3×10⁴, unconfounded DDA bias is ≤ 11% of the credit and vanishes by N = 10⁶ — the journey-level lemma survives cell-filling.
- *Discrete-U swap did not converge* once the total-lift constraint was added (max table diff 6e−3): 8 constraints on 8 parameters with a poor basin. Replaced (Round 3) by the continuous-U construction with a quadratic confounder term, which is exact.

**Round 3 (refine).** (i) Replaced the closed-form law with the operator law and the *Shapley-weighted* slope closed form (β̄_j = Σ_B w_j(B) β_{B∪j}), which is exact for the strongly targeted channel (ratio 1.01–1.05 at scale ≤ 0.2) and identifies the cross-leakage as the residual for the others. (ii) Replaced the box-Γ bounds by the channelwise model, which the first-order law motivates and which is 3× tighter at the true parameters; added the monotone variant and the robustness values. (iii) Made the swap exact with matched total and sign restrictions (E15). (iv) Derived the layered-design theorem (§5) from the pairwise closed form and tested it to n = 8.

**Round 4** re-ran the attacks against the refined constructions: the verifier found one boundary case in the design theorem (k = n with n odd, where the layer ⌊n/2⌋ is already the full factorial), fixed in the statement below; nothing else moved. Rounds 3→4 produced no material revision.

## 5. The experimental route: which holdout designs identify the causal Shapley allocation

A cell that suppresses channel set H on a random slice identifies v_pop(N∖H) = Σ_{T⊆N∖H} D(T) in the Möbius basis of v_pop. Sh_j(v_pop) = Σ_{T∋j} D(T)/|T| is identified iff its weight vector lies in the row space of the design's incidence matrix (with the ∅ column for the global-holdout intercept), optionally under a k-way interaction cap D(T) = 0 for |T| > k.

**Results (E7/E17; clean-room reproduced, n ≤ 8).** The global holdout plus single-channel holdouts (the ghost-ads menu) identify only τ_j and the total — never Sh for n ≥ 3, under any cap (rank 4/7 at n = 3). Define the *layered design* L_m = all cells with ≤ m channels held out or ≤ m channels exposed. Then under a k-way cap Sh(v_pop) is identified from L_m iff m ≥ min(⌈k/2⌉, ⌊n/2⌋) (verified exhaustively for n = 3…8, k = 2…7; the "only if" direction holds in every non-trivial case). In particular:

- **Pairwise interactions (k = 2): 2n + 2 cells suffice for any n** — global holdout, no holdout, n single-channel holdouts, n single-channel-only exposures — because 2-additive games satisfy the closed form **Sh_j = ½[v({j}) − v(∅) + v(N) − v(N∖j)] = ½(solo effect + removal effect)** (proved: Sh_j = m_j + ½Σ_i m_{ij}, and both bracketed differences are dividend sums; clean-room proved and checked on random n = 5 games to 4e−16). The causal Shapley credit under pairwise interactions is *the average of "the channel alone" and "the channel last"*. Its error under true 3-way dividends is d₁₂₃/6 per channel (uniform).
- Third-order cap: L₂ (58 of 128 cells at n = 7, 74 of 256 at n = 8); fourth-order: also L₂; fifth: L₃.

**Precision and cost (E8/E20).** At n = 3, N = 2M users spread over the 8 cells, the design's Shapley sd (0.0005) equals that of a two-arm single-channel lift test with the same N (0.90–0.98×): the causal Shapley allocation costs no more precision than one ghost-ad test, because each Sh_j is a ½-weighted sum of four cell means (|weight| row sum 2.0). At n = 4 the layered design (10 cells) beats the size-≤2-holdout design (12 cells) on precision (sd 0.00051 vs 0.00056) but forgoes more lift (51.5% vs 43.6% of the slice's lift, because "expose only j" cells suppress n − 1 channels); Neyman allocation across cells gains <2%. Removal effects alone (single holdouts) mis-allocate through interactions even when unconfounded: under overlapping reach (independent persuasion, E6) Σ τ_j falls short of the total by Σ(|T|−1)D(T) = −0.079 on 0.39 (20%); under synergy (θ₁₂ = 1) it over-allocates by 47%.

## 6. Verification and controls

- **Exactness.** All identities hold to ≤ 3e−17 (E1). Clean-room subagent (given definitions and claims only) reproduced Sh(v_pop), τ, DDA, the identity, the g = 0 lemma, the 2-additive closed form (with its own proof), the layered-design minimal m for n = 5…8, and the exact swap with B's parameters to 4 decimals.
- **Monte Carlo.** Journey-level DDA from finite panels is unbiased for its population value (E9: N = 10⁴/10⁵/10⁶, 30 reps; confounding bias exceeds sampling sd by 1.6–7.9× at N = 10⁴ and 16–103× at 10⁶). Seeds (E12, 5 seeds × 20 reps at N = 10⁵): means within 0.0004, sds 0.0006–0.0010.
- **Negative/power control (E10).** A global-holdout Hausman test of "DDA total = incremental total" (statistic Ȳ₀ − v̂(∅)): under no confounding it rejects at 4% (200 reps, N = 2×10⁵); under benchmark confounding t = 29.5 ± 1.1; under half confounding 16.9 ± 1.1. The instrument discriminates, and the experimental Shapley design recovers the truth (E8: mean (0.0230, 0.0220, 0.0001) vs truth (0.0230, 0.0220, 0)).
- **Robustness sweeps.** Link/confounder-distribution/heterogeneity (E11, 5 configs × 3 scales); targeting scale (E3, 7 levels); Γ-model variants (E5/E16, 4 regimes × 3 models); interaction strength (E18, 3 levels); sparse cells (E21, 3 N).
- **Uncertainty on headline numbers.** Exact quantities carry quadrature error < 1e−12 (stable 60→150 nodes per the verifier). MC-based: DDA sds above; design sds from 100–300 binomial reps (sd of sd ≈ 5%); the test's rejection rate at the null 0.04 ± 0.014 (200 reps).

## 7. Devil's advocate

- *"The impossibility is trivial — of course confounding breaks identification."* True as a slogan; the content is (a) that it survives monotone restrictions, a known total, and a smooth parametric family at n = 2 (exact swap), (b) that the only obstruction is the off-diagonal of the cross-journey table, which gives the sharp identified set and hence the bounds, and (c) the boundary found in E19: at n ≥ 3 parametric single-index families *are* over-identified by counting, so "attribution can be causal" is true in exactly the sense that Heckman selection is identified without an exclusion restriction. A critic could push the parametric route (E19's 7e−3 mismatch is detectable with 10⁶ users) — the reply is that the identification rests entirely on the Gaussian-logistic shape, which no one can test from the same data.
- *"DDA tools use paths, not sets, and regularized models, not cell means."* Ordering and repetition add unobserved cells but do not create an off-diagonal identification source; the principal-strata proof goes through verbatim with journeys as types. Regularization changes v̂_obs but not what it estimates.
- *"The bias law is only first-order."* Agreed, and it over-predicts by up to 2× at the benchmark; its role is to motivate the channelwise Γ model and give the sign and ordering, which the exact computations confirm (E2: monotone in g₃).
- *"Bounds at Γ_k* are tautologically covering."* Yes — the deliverable is the robustness value, not coverage. And the intervals are conservative (truth at 0.2–0.45 of the width), so a single-index LP would be sharper at the price of a parametric model.
- *"The experimental design is commercially unrealistic."* The "expose only j" cells suppress n − 1 channels on a slice; E20 quantifies the cost (≈50% of the slice's lift). The size-≤2-holdout alternative is milder and identifies the same thing at a 10% precision cost. Both need cross-platform holdout coordination that today exists only inside walled gardens — the mechanism problem E4/E6 of the catalog.
- *"Removal effects are what advertisers want, not Shapley."* Fine — but Σ τ_j ≠ total lift (−20% under reach overlap, +47% under synergy), so budgets allocated by removal effects don't add up; the closed form shows Shapley is one cheap cell-type away from what a ghost-ad program already runs.

## 8. Limitations and failure modes

Sets not paths; binary outcomes (the Γ odds model needs a bounded outcome; for revenue use Tan-style density-ratio bounds); a single latent confounder with logistic links in the numerical DGPs (the theorems are nonparametric, the bias law is not); the layered-design "iff" is exhaustive to n = 8, not proved beyond the k = 2 closed form; the robustness-value grid resolution is 0.01 in Γ; the swap search is a numerical least-squares construction (exact to 1e−17, but existence for general n is argued via the principal-strata proof, not constructed within the smooth family); no dynamic/adaptive delivery (dive 10's divergence bias compounds this one); costs in E20 ignore the platform-side cost of coordinating cells.

5. **Berman-style equilibrium with the exchange rate:** E14 gives ∂DDA_j/∂g_j ÷ ∂DDA_j/∂θ_j = 0.45–0.59 — a unit of targeting slope buys roughly half the credit of a unit of log-odds causal effect; embed in a publisher best-response model to quantify how much targeting effort attribution induces.
6. **Decision layer:** feed the [lo, hi] channelwise intervals into dive 08/09's act/hold protocol (also BL41 for dive 12's intervals).
