## 1. Problem statement

H1 says the chain attention → memory (category-entry-point links) → later purchase "has never been measured end to end" and that the solution is "longitudinal single-source panels linking attention measurement, CEP-based brand tracking, and purchase behavior on the same humans over years". The catalog names the asset but not what it would *identify* that geo experiments and MMMs cannot, nor how large it must be, nor what it costs. This dive treats the panel as a measurement instrument and asks three questions with explicit assumptions: (i) which objects in the chain are identified, under what randomization, by which contrast; (ii) the precision of each as a function of panel size N, waves W, wave spacing Δ, survey reliability and category purchase frequency — the SNR ladder; (iii) a cost model and the resulting minimal design. The honest answer turns out to be asymmetric: the panel identifies the *memory* half of the chain at an SNR an order of magnitude above anything sales data can give, while the *purchase* half sits exactly in Lewis–Rao's unfavorable regime and is rescued only by design features (a pre-period, a second randomized memory instrument, high-frequency categories), not by the panel's existence.

Formalization (explicit assumptions):

- **Units.** N persons, weeks t = 1..T (T = 104 in the base design). Half are randomized to an ad *holdout* Z_i = 0 (addressable suppression on the panelist's devices), half exposed (A1: randomized exposure at the person level is available — CTV/digital addressability or split-cable; without it nothing below the memory contrast is identified).
- **Attention.** Impressions per exposed person per week ~ Poisson(μ_t) on a flighted schedule (6 weeks on / 7 off, μ = 3); attentive seconds A_it = Σ over impressions of Gamma-distributed dwell with a *persistent* person propensity a_i ~ Gamma(2, 1) (mean 2 s/impression). A_it is what a device-level attention instrument (camera/eye tracking) measures.
- **Memory.** Latent M_it = m_i + x_it with a stable trait m_i ~ N(0, 1) and a transient x_it = λ x_{i,t−1} + κ A_it + η_it, η ~ N(0, 0.15²), λ = 0.90 (half-life 6.6 weeks), κ = 0.012 (an attentive second adds 0.012 memory units) — the exponential-decay analogue of an adstock, but on a *measured* state. Interpretation: M is the person's brand–CEP link strength on a scale where the cross-sectional sd of the survey score is ≈ 1.3.
- **Survey.** S_it = M_it + e_it, e ~ N(0, 0.7²), only at wave weeks (every Δ = 8 weeks, W = 12 waves); cross-sectional reliability ≈ 0.72. Optional **conditioning**: taking the survey adds δ to x (the survey is itself a memory refresh, δ = 0.3 in the attacked runs), and a **rotating** design surveys a random half of panelists at each wave.
- **Purchase.** Category occasions ~ Poisson(f) per week (f = 0.5, ≈ 26 occasions/year; swept 0.05–1.5); brand chosen with probability expit(α_i + b M_it + d A_it), b = 0.25, d = 0 (a *direct* path d > 0 is the alternative the mediation tests must detect), α_i = −2 + 1.0·(0.5 z1 + √0.75 z2) so that purchase propensity correlates 0.5 with the memory trait (brand users remember more). Optional **feedback** ψ: a purchase adds ψ to memory (usage refreshes memory). Base numbers: ≈ 4.4 brand purchases/person/year, exposed-arm lift ≈ 5.4% (E15), 2-year purchase CV ≈ 0.93.
- **Targets.** (T1) the exposure → memory kernel (κ, λ) — the object adstock conventions proxy for; (T2) β_Y := ∂E[Y_it]/∂M_it, the purchase response to memory (= f·b·p(1−p) averaged; truth 0.0152/week/unit); (T3) the share of the purchase effect mediated by measured memory; (T4) the sales-fitted geometric adstock α versus the memory λ.

## 2. Prior state

Literature subagent (this run, 25 searches). Every existing single-source asset covers at most two of the three nodes: BehaviorScan split-cable panels (≈ 3,000 households/market; Lodish et al. 1995, 389 tests, half significant; Lodish et al. 1995b on persistence, and > 150 cessation tests of which just over half showed no significant effect) and Jones's STAS panel (2,000 households, TV meters + scanners, 7-day window, contested by Ehrenberg/Broadbent/McDonald on the buying-frequency confound) link *exposure and purchase* with no memory node; NCS (claimed 1.4M households), Kantar Worldpanel (30,000 UK households + media survey), Numerator (200,000-household static panel) are exposure-proxy + purchase; Amplified Intelligence's 3,000–5,000-person attention panel routes exposed panelists to a simulated shelf (attention → *stated* intent); Lumen (PwC audit: 70% viewed/not-viewed accuracy), Adelaide (a placement score, not a panel) and TVision have no verified-purchase linkage; the ARF Attention Validation Initiative (12 providers, 32 ads, 2024) found convergent validity *inconsistent across method classes*, so "attention" is not yet an interchangeable unit. Mindset-metric econometrics (Srinivasan–Vanhuele–Pauwels 2010: awareness/consideration/liking explain "almost one-third" of explained sales variance across 60+ brands; Hanssens et al. 2014's potential/responsiveness/stickiness/conversion criteria; Ataman–van Heerde–Mela 2010: long-run advertising elasticity 0.13 vs 1.37 for product) is aggregate VAR evidence, not individual-level mediation. Decay: Leone 1995's 6–9-month 90% duration; Zielske 1959's recall decay; Sethuraman–Tellis–Briesch 2011 (mean elasticity 0.12, 53% significant). Power/economics: Lewis & Rao 2015 (25 experiments; purchase CV ≈ 10; median ROI CI > 100 points; "more than ten million person-weeks"); ghost ads (Johnson–Lewis–Nubbemeyer 2017); Gordon et al. 2019 (observational methods badly biased vs 15 Facebook RCTs). Methodology: Imai–Keele–Yamamoto 2010 sequential ignorability and its ρ sensitivity parameter; Fritz & MacKinnon 2007 (N = 72 for medium/medium mediation, hundreds–thousands for small); Raudenbush & Liu 2001 (waves vs subjects); Kenny & Zautra's STARTS model (stable trait + autoregressive trait + state; ≥ 4 waves for identification) and the response-latency reliability critique (implicit measures test–retest r < 0.4). The subagent flagged five gaps: no study instruments all three nodes on identified persons over months; no Prentice-sense surrogacy analysis of a memory metric for long-run sales; no published test–retest reliability for CEP-link surveys; no independent purchase-linked validation of any attention vendor; and — the one this dive attacks first — *nobody has tested whether the sales-fitted adstock decay and an independently measured memory decay agree on the same population*.

Two literatures the subagent did not surface but that this dive's design leans on: the **mere-measurement effect** (Morwitz, Johnson & Schmittlein 1993; Chandon, Morwitz & Reinartz 2005 — measuring purchase intentions raises subsequent purchase of durables, and repeated measurement polarizes) is prior art that a *survey is a memory intervention*; and the Heckman "heterogeneity vs state dependence" problem is the reason tracking-only decay estimates fail (§3.2).

## 3. Constructions

### 3.1 Construction 1 — the identification ladder (derived here; the levels' precisions are the dive's main numbers)

With person-level randomized exposure and repeated memory surveys, four contrasts are available, each identifying a different object:

**Level A — the memory kernel (κ, λ) from wave contrasts.** E[S_iw | Z=1] − E[S_iw | Z=0] = κ Σ_{j≥0} λ^j E[A_{w−j}], a convolution of the *known* expected attention path with the kernel. Weighted NLS on the 12 wave contrasts recovers (κ, λ) with no stationarity assumption, no reliance on natural variation, and no purchase data. E1 (N = 3,000, 40 reps): λ̂ = 0.896 (MC sd 0.012; linearized se 0.017, conservative), κ̂ = 0.0124 (sd 0.0015, truth 0.012); a single wave's memory contrast has |t| = 6.7 on average (max 9.6). Half-life 6.6 ± 0.75 weeks from 3,000 people. Clean-room: λ̂ 0.8998 (sd 0.0072), κ̂ 0.01226 (sd 0.00136), |t| 6.94.

**Level B — the purchase kernel scale and the total effect.** The same randomization gives the weekly purchase contrast diff_t = E[Y_t|Z=1] − E[Y_t|Z=0]. Under pure memory mediation diff_t = c·shape_t with shape_t the Level-A memory path and c = β_Y — so the purchase contrast is regressed on a *shape measured at Level A* and only a scalar is estimated (dive 12's known-shape/unknown-scalar trick, with the shape now measured on humans rather than assumed). The estimator is a matched filter: c = Σ_t w_t diff_t is a difference of arm means of the person statistic q_i = Σ_t w_t Y_it, so its se is exact. E1: c = 0.0133 (sd 0.0074, t 1.58) versus the plain 2-year total effect 0.42 purchases (se 0.30, t 1.39; clean-room se 0.29–0.30, sd of 2-year purchases 8.23 vs 8.25). This is Lewis–Rao's regime: the *same* experiment that measures memory at 7σ per wave measures purchases at 1.4σ over two years.

**Level C — a second, ad-free instrument for memory (the rotating survey).** If taking the survey refreshes memory by δ (the mere-measurement/panel-conditioning effect), then randomizing *which half* of the panel is surveyed at each wave creates N·W/2 person-level randomized memory bumps that carry no ad content. Identification: δ·λ^Δ = E[S_{i,w+1} | surveyed at w] − E[S_{i,w+1} | not] (randomized, so a plain contrast, using Level A's λ); β_Y^{survey} = (within-person contrast of purchases in the Δ−1 weeks after a survey between surveyed and unsurveyed waves) / (δ Σ_{k=1}^{Δ−1} λ^k). Two-way (person × wave) demeaning removes the trait correlation. E4 (δ = 0.3, N = 3,000): δ̂ = 0.307 (sd 0.080), β_Y^{survey} = 0.0147 (sd 0.0057, t 2.2) versus β_Y^{ads} = 0.0162 (sd 0.0062, t 2.0) and truth 0.0152; with δ = 0 the design correctly returns δ̂ = 0.003 (sd 0.059) and the instrument is void. Two design consequences: the rotating half-sample halves survey cost, and the conditioning bias that a fixed-panel design suffers silently becomes an *estimated* quantity.

**Level D — what is not identified.** Write the purchase kernel as h_Y = β_Y h_M + h_⊥ where h_⊥ collects unmeasured parallel paths (Vakratsas–Ambler's non-hierarchical processes). Level A gives h_M, Level B gives h_Y; proportionality h_Y ∝ h_M is *necessary* for pure mediation but not sufficient — a parallel path with the same decay is invisible. Hence from Levels A–B alone the mediated share s = β_Y Σh_M/Σh_Y is only bounded above by the proportional part of h_Y, and bounded below by 0. Level C breaks the tie: under the survey's exclusion restriction (a survey affects purchase only through memory), β_Y^{survey} point-identifies β_Y, so s is point-identified and the *equality* β_Y^{survey} = β_Y^{ads} becomes a testable implication of pure mediation. This is the formal content of "measuring the mechanism": the panel converts an unidentified mediated share into a point, at the price of one exclusion restriction that the ad instrument does not need.

### 3.2 Construction 2 — the negative result on tracking-only decay (heterogeneity masquerading as persistence)

The obvious cheap alternative — estimate λ from the autocorrelation of the tracking survey without an experiment (a quasi-simplex/STARTS fit c(k) = V_m + V_x φ^k to the between-wave cross-sectional covariances, ≥ 4 waves) — is biased upward and 3–7× noisier. E2 (20 reps): pooled tracking φ^{1/8} = 0.929 (sd 0.035), arm-demeaned 0.915 (sd 0.037), exposed-only 0.926 (sd 0.056), holdout-only 0.889 (sd 0.078), experimental 0.892 (sd 0.010). Clean-room: 0.938 (sd 0.033) pooled, 0.902 (sd 0.064) holdout. The mechanism is that heavy attenders (persistent a_i) accumulate persistently higher memory across flights, and a covariance decomposition reads that persistence as a slower decay — Heckman's heterogeneity-vs-state-dependence problem on the memory state. The holdout arm, which has no exposure-driven heterogeneity, is unbiased but has so little transient variance that its reliability collapses (0.60–0.72 → sd 0.078). Consequence for H5's "regression to the mean" debate: a tracker without randomized exposure cannot separate memory persistence from audience persistence, at any N.

### 3.3 Construction 3 — sales-fitted adstock ≠ memory decay (derived here; clean-room exact)

If the true sales impulse response is a mixture h_k = ω·1[k=0] + (1−ω)(1−λ)λ^k (a share ω of the effect is an instantaneous retrieval/direct path, the rest is memory), the geometric adstock an MMM fits by least squares has pseudo-true decay

| λ \ ω | 0 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 | 0.80 | 0.73 | 0.60 | 0.40 | 0.17 |
| 0.9 | 0.90 | 0.847 | 0.548 | 0.253 | 0.096 |
| 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.915 | 0.238 | 0.124 | 0.050 |

(E7; clean-room identical to 3–4 digits). A 20% direct share collapses the fitted half-life from 6.6 to 1.2 weeks: the geometric family cannot represent a spike plus a tail, and least squares, dominated by lag 0, sacrifices the tail. In the finite-sample MMM version (E7b: N = 20,000 aggregate weekly sales, flighted spend, profile over α), the pure-memory world returns α = 0.904 ± 0.018 but the 20%-direct world returns 0.80 ± 0.10 — between the two pseudo-true values and on a flat ridge (dive 01's δ⁴ ridge). So the sales-fitted α is *neither* the memory half-life nor a stable pseudo-true value; the panel's λ (Level A) is the only one of the three that is a property of humans rather than of the estimator. This is the specific sense in which H1 "validates or overturns the adstock conventions": the test is α̂_MMM vs λ̂_panel, and a gap is *expected* whenever a direct path exists — its size measures ω.

### 3.4 Construction 4 — design laws (derived here; the Fisher law clean-room exact, the finite-sample corrections MC)

**Wave spacing.** After a single burst the memory contrast decays as D₀λ^τ; with W waves at lags 0, Δ, …, (W−1)Δ and noise 2σ_S²/N per wave, the linearized se(λ̂) is minimized at Δ = 11, 8, 6, 4 weeks for W = 2, 3, 4, 6 (se 0.0092, 0.0073, 0.0064, 0.0055 at D₀ = 1; clean-room exact), i.e. the *span* (W−1)Δ converges to ≈ 2 memory time-constants (2/(−ln λ) = 19 weeks) and extra waves inside the span buy little (W 2 → 6 cuts se 1.7× for 3× the surveys); the Box–Lucas D-optimal point 1/(−ln λ) = 9.5 weeks sits at the W = 2 optimum. But E3's Monte Carlo of the 3-wave burst design at realistic D₀ = 0.34 shows the Fisher law understates the finite-sample sd 1.5–2× at Δ ≥ 6 (sd 0.034/0.041/0.050/0.168 at Δ = 3/6/10/16 vs theory 0.030/0.022/0.022/0.029) and that large spacings have heavy tails (Δ = 16: mean 0.845, MAD-sd 0.044). **Refinement:** repeated flights with regular waves dominate — E3b at a *fixed budget* (cost = N·(300 + W·4 $)): W = 4 (N = 3,303) gives λ sd 0.054 and a void survey instrument (t 0.5); W = 6 (3,222): 0.033 / t 1.0; W = 12 (3,000): 0.019 / t 2.2; W = 24 (2,636): 0.016 / t 2.2. The knee is Δ ≈ one time constant (8 weeks); halving Δ again is neutral.

**Pre-period.** Because purchase heterogeneity is persistent (α_i), each panelist's own pre-campaign purchase rate is the strongest covariate available. E14 (flights start at week 30; outcome weeks 30–129): the total-effect t rises 1.47 → 1.67 → 1.86 → 2.22 with 0/4/13/26 pre-weeks (gain 1.51× in t, ≈ 2.3× in N-equivalent; clean-room 1.96 → 2.70, gain 1.38×). Six months of watching people buy before the first ad is worth doubling the panel.

**Matched filter.** Regressing the weekly contrast on the Level-A shape rather than summing purchases gains only 1.58/1.39 = 1.14× in t under the base flighting (the schedule covers most weeks); the gain is √(T Σs²)/Σs and grows with sparser flighting.

## 4. Attack-and-refine history

**Round 1 (construct).** Levels A–D, the tracking negative result, the adstock table, the Fisher spacing law and a first cost model.

**Round 2 (attack) — what broke.**
1. *The Fisher spacing law is optimistic and the burst design is fragile* (E3 MC vs theory: 1.5–2× and tail blow-up). Answered in Round 3 by replacing the single-burst optimum with the fixed-budget flighted comparison (E3b), which is what the writeup recommends.
2. *The two-instrument equality test has no power at feasible N.* E6: with a 20% direct share the ad instrument reads 0.0197 vs the survey's 0.0147 (N = 3,000) — a 25–34% gap — but z̄ = 0.49 and the rejection rate is 0–2.5% at N = 3,000–10,000. The equality is a valid implication but not a usable test. Round 3 answered with the shape-transfer test (E5), which detects a direct path by its *timing* rather than its scale: a 17% direct share rejects at 15%/40%/88% (N = 3k/10k/30k), a 35% share at 65%/95%/100%; null rejection 0.05–0.10 (E5b, 60 reps: 0.067 at 3k, 0.10 at 30k — mildly anti-conservative because the shape is estimated).
3. *Shape misspecification manufactures a direct path.* With heterogeneous decay (λ ∈ {0.7, 0.97}, half each) the one-exponential shape from Level A fits the slow component (λ̂ = 0.962, E10) and the fast component's residual loads on the lag-0 term: false rejection 0.183 at N = 30,000 (E5b). Round 3: fit a two-exponential kernel to the wave contrasts (identifiable at N = 30,000: components 0.69–0.79 and 0.97 recovered; weakly at 3,000) and transfer *that* shape — false rejection falls to 0.05 with no loss under the base model (0.117 vs 0.10).
4. *The observational within-person regression is worthless.* E8: OLS of purchases on the measured memory score with person and wave fixed effects gives 0.0016 vs truth 0.0152 — 9.5× attenuation (clean-room 7.75×) — because the *within-person* reliability of the memory score is 0.19 (clean-room 0.190) even though the cross-sectional reliability is 0.72: within a person the transient memory variance (sd 0.34 + exposure lift) is small against survey error (0.7). With purchase → memory feedback ψ = 0.3/0.6 the OLS slope turns *negative* (−0.0018/−0.0037; even on the true M, −0.007/−0.0095), while both randomized instruments stay on target (survey 0.0140/0.0153, ads 0.0156/0.0153). The panel's value is in its randomizations, not in having both variables on the same person.
5. *Attrition.* Per-wave dropout hazards of ≈ 10% that depend on the memory trait or on purchase propensity (retention 28–34% at two years) leave (κ, λ) unbiased (E9: λ 0.891–0.898, κ 0.0121–0.0124) because dropout is independent of Z; the completer population differs from the recruited one (a transport, not a validity, problem).
6. *Misspecification sweeps* (E10). Saturating encoding: λ̂ 0.883 (sd 0.035), κ̂ 0.0086 (−30%; the kernel is the small-dose tangent), β estimates intact. Mis-estimated expected attention (×1.3): κ̂ rescales exactly (0.0089 = 0.0116/1.3), λ̂ and both β's unchanged — the attention instrument's *level* calibration is irrelevant to decay and to the purchase link, only its relative timing matters. Survey noise ×2: λ sd 0.022 → 0.035; ÷2: 0.018 — reliability is a second-order lever compared with N and Δ.

**Round 3 (refine).** Items 1–3 above; plus the mediated-share estimator (E13): pure mediation → median 1.01 (IQR 0.81–1.29) at N = 10,000 and 0.97 (0.88–1.09) at 30,000; 20% direct share (truth 0.80 from E15) → 0.84 (0.65–0.94) and 0.78 (0.70–0.87). Point-identified, but a ±0.1 answer needs 30,000 people.

**Round 4 (attack again).** The pre-period gain was re-examined after the clean-room disagreed (mine 2.5×, theirs 1.38×): my E14 had summed the pre-period weeks into the outcome for the unadjusted arm; restricting the outcome to campaign weeks gives 1.51× (E14 as now coded). No other revision; Rounds 3 → 4 produced no material change to the constructions, and the writeup was frozen.

## 6. The minimal panel: SNR ladder, sizing and cost

**SNR ladder (E11, two years, 12 rotating waves, Δ = 8; t-statistics).**

| category f (purch./yr) | N | t(λ) | t(β_Y ads) | t(β_Y survey) | t(total) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 (0.46) | 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 | 23 / 38 / 72 | 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.1 | 0.7 / 0.8 / 1.0 | 0.4 / 0.9 / 2.0 |
| 0.2 (1.8) | 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 | 24 / 39 / 73 | 0.8 / 1.6 / 2.7 | 0.9 / 1.4 / 2.1 | 0.8 / 1.6 / 2.6 |
| 0.5 (4.6) | 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 | 23 / 38 / 72 | 1.1 / 1.9 / 3.3 | 1.4 / 2.1 / 4.1 | 1.0 / 1.9 / 3.0 |
| 1.5 (13.7) | 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 | 22 / 40 / 71 | 1.4 / 2.5 / 4.1 | 1.8 / 3.5 / 6.3 | 1.3 / 2.4 / 3.9 |

The memory kernel is category-independent and 20–70σ; the purchase link scales with √(N·f). For 80% power (t ≈ 2.8) at a ≈ 5% lift, √N scaling gives: frequent CPG (f = 0.5) N ≈ 7,400 (ads) / 4,800 (survey), ≈ 3,300 with a 26-week pre-period on the ad instrument; weekly-purchase categories (f = 1.5) N ≈ 2,000–4,700; infrequent categories (f = 0.05) N ≈ 18,000 (ads, ≈ 8,000 with pre-period) and the survey instrument is useless (72,000). The survey instrument overtakes the ad instrument as f rises because it is within-person and repeats N·W/2 times.

**Cost model (E12; component prices are assumptions, not quotes: recruit $40; attention device/panel $120/yr; verified purchase capture $60/yr; incentive $60/yr; survey complete $8).** Per person for a 2-year panel with 12 rotating waves: $568; N = 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 / 30,000 → $0.57M / $1.7M / $5.7M / $17M. Two minimal designs follow:

- **Design M (memory only, tests the adstock convention):** N ≈ 500, one year, 6–8 waves at Δ = 8 after ≥ 2 flights, randomized holdout — se(λ̂) ≈ 0.03 (half-life ± 2 weeks), κ at ≈ 3σ, cost ≈ $0.2–0.3M. Delivers Level A and the α_MMM-vs-λ test (§3.3) for one brand; cannot say anything about purchase.
- **Design P (the H1 panel):** N ≈ 3,500 in a frequent CPG category (or 8,000–10,000 in an infrequent one), 26-week purchase-only pre-period, then two years of flighted exposure with randomized holdout, 12 rotating memory waves (half-sample, Δ = 8 weeks), device-measured attention on the exposed arm — ≈ $2.2M (3,500 × $568 + pre-period capture). Delivers Levels A–C: (κ, λ) at 40σ, β_Y at ≈ 2.8σ from each instrument (≈ 4σ pooled), the shape-transfer test with 65–95% power against a 35% direct path, and a mediated-share point estimate with IQR ≈ ±0.15. A ±0.1 mediated share or an 88%-power test of a 17% direct path needs N ≈ 30,000 ($17M).

What the panel *cannot* do at any of these sizes: detect the purchase effect of a single exposure (Lewis–Rao), separate a parallel path with the same decay from memory mediation without the survey exclusion, or identify individual-level heterogeneity in β_Y (H2).

## 7. Devil's advocate against each headline claim

*"The memory kernel is identified at 40σ from 3,000 people."* Only if exposure is person-randomizable on the panelist's devices and the expected attention path is known up to scale (E10 shows scale errors are harmless, timing errors are not); a panel that measures attention but cannot suppress ads is back to §3.2's biased tracking. Also, the model's memory is a one-dimensional linear state; if CEP links are many and saturate (E10 bounded: κ̂ −30%), "κ" is a small-dose tangent and "λ" a dominant-mode decay (E10 λ-mixture: 0.962, the slow component) — still a property of humans, but not the parameter a single-λ adstock means.

*"Tracking-only decay is biased upward."* The sign depends on the heterogeneity being in *exposure response* (persistent attenders); heterogeneity in survey error would bias nothing, and mean-reverting exposure (audiences that switch) could bias downward. The claim is that the bias is unsigned and unbounded without an experiment, not that it is always upward.

*"MMM adstock ≠ memory half-life."* The pseudo-true table assumes white-noise spend weighting; under flighted spend the MMM sits on a ridge (0.80 ± 0.10) and might, by luck, land near λ. The claim that survives is that agreement is uninformative and disagreement is expected under a direct path.

*"The survey is a second instrument."* This is the speculative step: it needs (a) a measurable δ (the mere-measurement literature says intentions questions move durable purchases; whether a CEP-link battery moves CPG memory by a usable δ is untested — with δ = 0.1 the instrument's t falls 3×), and (b) the exclusion restriction, which the same literature's "self-generated validity" mechanism arguably violates in the *same* direction as memory (the survey raises accessibility → purchase), making the two hard to distinguish. It is offered as the only known way to point-identify the mediated share, not as established.

*"3,500 people and $2.2M suffice."* For a 5% lift in a frequent category with a 26-week pre-period. Halve the lift and N quadruples; drop the pre-period and it doubles; move to a quarterly-purchase category and it triples. The cost prices are assumptions; a device-based attention panel at $120/person-year may be optimistic by 2×.

## 8. Limitations and failure modes

Linear-Gaussian memory with a single decay; purchase as independent Poisson occasions (no inter-purchase timing, no stockpiling); no cross-person interference; the holdout is clean (no ghost-ad non-compliance, cf. BL48); no seasonality in baseline purchase (which the pre-period covariate and the randomization absorb, but which interacts with the matched filter's weights); attention measured without error on the exposed arm and the expected attention path known (the level is irrelevant, the timing is not); a single brand and campaign, so κ is confounded with creative quality (H4) and cannot be transported; the shape-transfer test is mildly anti-conservative (0.07–0.10) because the shape is estimated — a bootstrap over the wave contrasts would fix this; the two-exponential refinement is weakly identified at N = 3,000; mediated-share estimates are ratios of noisy quantities and their medians are biased at N ≤ 10,000; the equality test is powerless (E6) and should be reported as a consistency check only.

## 9. Next steps for a future session

1. **Nonlinear memory state:** replace the AR(1) with a bounded CEP-link count (binomial links with per-link decay) and ask what "λ" the Level-A contrast estimates; does the α_MMM-vs-λ test survive when the true kernel is not exponential?
2. **The δ pilot:** a design to measure the CEP-survey refresh δ cheaply (rotating survey, memory-only, N ≈ 500) before committing to Design P — and a sensitivity analysis (Imai–Keele–Yamamoto ρ) for the survey's exclusion restriction.
3. **Attention as an instrumented dose:** randomize format (skippable/non-skippable) within the exposed arm to get a second dose level and test whether κ is linear in attentive seconds — the H3 "attention-to-encoding" question as a two-dose design.
4. **Join to dive 12:** the Level-A kernel is the shape dive 12's known-shape/unknown-scalar trick needs; a panel plus a 52-lag observational sales panel would de-confound the long-run effect with a *measured* shape — compute the (G, H, N) frontier.
5. **Join to dive 02:** transport (κ, λ) into an MMM as an informative prior on adstock via the operator likelihood; quantify what the panel is worth in MMM decision loss (dive 03's machinery) — the value-of-information case for Design M.
